What the paper contributes

A machine-checked formalization, in Lean 4 / Mathlib, of Jacobi's formula and Newton's identity for matrix traces, composed with Bass's formula. The mathematics is classical; the contribution is the formalization.

  • Jacobi's formula in its algebraic form, (det M)′ = tr(adj M · M′) for a matrix of polynomials over a commutative ring (Theorem 1), for which the paper found no prior proof in any proof assistant. Axioms: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
  • Newton's identity for matrix traces to all orders, eigenvalue-free: (charpolyRev M)′ = −charpolyRev M · Σk tr(Mk+1) Xk (Theorem 2), through the resolvent series (I − XM)−1 = Σk MkXk over formal power series (Lemma 1).
  • Matrix powers count walks, also for directed graphs (Lemma 2), with the trace rewritten over cyclic walks and its weight invariant under rotation.
  • Composed with Bass's formula: the generating function of the non-backtracking counts Nk = tr(Bk) from the adjacency data (A, D) (Theorem 3), and tr(Bk) counts rooted closed non-backtracking walks (Theorem 4).
  • The boundary, as the paper states it: rooted walks, not geodesic classes; the prime-geodesic Euler product is mapped, not built.

Main results:

  • Theorem 1: Jacobi's formula: (det M)′ = tr(adj M · M′) for a matrix of polynomials
  • Theorem 2: Newton's identity for matrix traces: (charpolyRev M)′ = −charpolyRev M · Σ tr(Mᵏ⁺¹)Xᵏ
  • Theorem 3: the Ihara Nₖ identity: Σ tr(Bᵏ⁺¹)Xᵏ from the adjacency data (A, D)

🧭 Fields: where the paper lives and what it connects

Tools taken from other branches

  • Linear algebra → the Leibniz formula in product form, Cramer’s rule read off the adjugate, adj(I − XM) = det(I − XM)·(I − XM)⁻¹ — Theorem 1, Theorem 2
  • Formal power series → the resolvent series Σ MᵏXᵏ as the inverse of I − XM, from the fixed-point equation R = I + XM·R; units of constant term 1 — 1, Lemma 1, Theorem 2, Theorem 3
  • Graph combinatorics → walks of a decidable relation, cyclic walks and rotation of the basepoint; darts and the non-backtracking relation — Lemma 2, Theorem 4
