The Clock That Never Ticks: a machine-checked path from the dilogarithm to quantum speed limits in Lean 4

🏷️ Special functions · the dilogarithm and the Clausen function

Carles Marín

At a glance

What the paper contributes

A sorry-free Lean 4 path from the dilogarithm, which Mathlib cites but does not define, to a quantum state that never orthogonalizes.

  • The real dilogarithm with Euler’s reflection, Landen’s transformation, the duplication formula and Li₂(1/2) (Theorem 1); the golden-ratio values and the Lee–Yang ceff = 2/5 from a 3×3 linear system, without the five-term relation.
  • The circle: the Clausen function and Catalan’s constant G = Cl₂(π/2); the Fejér–Jackson inequality (Theorem 2) and, by Abel summation without asymptotics, Cl₂(θ) ≥ sin(θ)/2 on (0, π).
  • Speed limits: Margolus–Levitin and an L¹ form of Mandelstam–Tamm for a scalar spectral measure (Theorem 3).
  • The assembly (Theorem 4): populations 6/(π²n²) on levels Eₙ = n give infinite mean energy and variance, so both bounds are silent, and yet the state never reaches an orthogonal state.
  • The mathematics is classical; the paper claims the formalization and the assembly, and records the Eneström–Kakeya reading as an observation (Remark 1).

📐 Setting (copied from the paper, §1)

The file Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Integrals/LogTrigonometric.lean of Mathlib … describes one of its integrals by referring to the dilogarithm

\[ {\mathrm{Li}_2}(x) \; =\; \sum _{n\ge 1}\frac{x^n}{n^2}. \]

(§3) In the Rogers normalization \(\operatorname{L}(x)={\mathrm{Li}_2}(x)+\tfrac 12\ln x\ln (1-x)\) the \(\ln ^2\) defects cancel. (§4) On the circle the dilogarithm splits into a tame half and a wild half:

\[ {\mathrm{Li}_2}(e^{i\theta }) \; =\; \underbrace{\sum _{n\ge 1}\frac{\cos n\theta }{n^2}}_{\textstyle =\; \frac{\pi ^2}{6}-\frac{\pi \theta }{2}+\frac{\theta ^2}{4}} \; +\; i\, \underbrace{\sum _{n\ge 1}\frac{\sin n\theta }{n^2}}_{\textstyle =\; {\mathrm{Cl}_2}(\theta )} \qquad (\theta \in [0,2\pi ]). \]

(§6) A state \(\lvert \psi _0\rangle =\sum _n c_n\lvert E_n\rangle \) evolves with autocorrelation \(S(t)=\sum _n p_n e^{-iE_nt}\), \(p_n=\lvert c_n\rvert ^2\), and reaches an orthogonal state at time \(\tau \) iff \(S(\tau )=0\). Take populations \(p_n=\frac{6}{\pi ^2 n^2}\) on levels \(E_n=n\): the weight-2 zeta state.

📕 p. 8📍 map❝ cite⚠ report

For monotone non-increasing populations on equally spaced levels, ticking (orthogonalization) means a zero of the power series \(\sum _n p_nz^n\) on the unit circle. The Eneström–Kakeya theorem (1893/1912)  [ 12 ] already forbids zeros inside the disc; its boundary case pins down the rest. From \((1-z)\sum _k p_kz^k=p_0+\sum _{k\ge 1}(p_k-p_{k-1})z^k-p_Nz^{N+1}\), the triangle inequality at \(\lvert z\rvert =1\) is an equality only if every nonzero decrement \(p_{k-1}-p_k\) sits at an index \(k\) with \(z^k=1\): the decrements must concentrate on an arithmetic progression, and the zero is a root of unity. The Margolus–Levitin–sharp state \((\lvert 0\rangle +\lvert E\rangle )/\sqrt2\) is exactly such a case (\(\frac12(1+z)\), zero at \(z=-1\)). The zeta state is the opposite extreme: its successive decrements \(p_n-p_{n+1}{\gt}0\) at every index, never confined to a progression, so no root of unity survives and the clock cannot tick — recovering Theorem 4 from the coefficient side. We have not found the resulting dictionary — root location of positive-coefficient series \(\leftrightarrow \) orthogonalizability of monotone quantum states — in either literature; we record it as an observation and a road.
