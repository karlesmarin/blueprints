What the paper contributes

The quadratic persistence of the Segre varieties $\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^2$ and $\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^3$, exactly, and a block bound in every width. Through the Pythagoras-number bound of Blekherman, Sinn, Smith and Velasco this shows that the sum-of-squares rank $\mathrm{BSR}(m,n)$ of biquadratic forms is not captured by the graph parameters $z_2$, $z_{2A}$ used to bound it: the difference grows linearly in $m$ in every width $n\ge4$.

  • $\mathrm{qp}(\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^2)=m$ and $\mathrm{qp}(\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^3)=\lceil4m/3\rceil$ (Theorems 1 and 2); $\mathrm{qp}\le n\lceil m/3\rceil$ (Theorem 3).
  • $\mathrm{BSR}(m,4)\ge\lfloor8m/3\rfloor$, above $z_2(m,4)=z_{2A}(m,4)$ for $m=21$ and $m\ge23$ (Corollary 4); a gap of order $\frac{n-3}6m$ in width $n$ (Corollary 5).
  • Rational forms whose rational length exceeds their real length by exactly three (Theorem 6).
  • Exact persistence in widths five and six for $m\le18$, $m\le14$ (Proposition 7), and $8\le\mathrm{BSR}(4,3)\le9$ (Theorem 9).

Main results:

  • Theorem 2: qp of Pᵐ⁻¹ × P³ is ⌈4m/3⌉
  • Theorem 3: block bound qp ≤ n⌈m/3⌉ in every width
  • Corollary 4: BSR(m,4) ≥ ⌊8m/3⌋, above z₂ and z₂A from m = 21
  • Theorem 9: BSR(m,n) ≤ mn − max(m,n) + 1, so 8 ≤ BSR(4,3) ≤ 9
