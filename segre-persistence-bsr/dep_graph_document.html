Remark 8

If \(\operatorname{qp}({\mathbb {P}}^{m-1}\times {\mathbb {P}}^{n-1})=s_0(m,n)\) for all \(m\), as these computations suggest for \(n=5,6\), then \({\mathrm{BSR}}(m,n)/m\) is at least \(1+\sqrt{(n-1)(n-2)/2}\) asymptotically, that is \(1+\sqrt6\approx 3.45\) for \(n=5\) and \(1+\sqrt{10}\approx 4.16\) for \(n=6\), against \((n+1)/2\) for the cell bound. For \(n=4\) the same expression gives \(1+\sqrt3\), above the slope \(8/3\) that Theorem 2 actually gives.

Corollary 4

\({\mathrm{BSR}}(m,3)\ge 2m\) and \({\mathrm{BSR}}(m,4)\ge \lfloor 8m/3\rfloor \). For \(m\ge 15\),

\[ {\mathrm{BSR}}(m,4)-z_2(m,4)\ \ge \ \Bigl\lfloor \frac{8m}3\Bigr\rfloor -\Bigl\lfloor \frac{5m+6}2\Bigr\rfloor \ \ge \ \frac{m-22}6. \]

The last expression is positive for \(m\ge 23\), and the middle one equals \(56-55=1\) at \(m=21\) and \(0\) at \(m=22\). The same bounds hold with \(z_{2A}\) in place of \(z_2\).
