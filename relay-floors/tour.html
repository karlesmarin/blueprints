🎯 The result 1 / 5

Theorem 4.1.

Under H1–H2 with \(\kappa \ge \gamma \), for every control satisfying (1) and every \(T{\gt}0\),

\begin{equation} \boxed {P(T)\le \frac{e^{-\gamma T}}{1+(\kappa -\gamma )/(G^2T)}} \end{equation}

💬 In plain words 2 / 5

A transfer has two ways to lose the excitation. If it goes slowly, the qubits decay: that is the factor $e^{-\gamma T}$. If it goes fast, the excitation must pass through the bus, and the bus loses more: with couplers of strength $G$, moving the excitation in time $T$ forces the bus to hold it for an integrated time of at least $\tilde P/(G^2T)$ (Cauchy–Schwarz), and the bus loses at the excess rate $\kappa-\gamma$ while it holds it. No pulse escapes both. The bound holds for every shape, phase and duration and for any number of bus modes, because the proof only uses the three inequalities (3) of the Setting. For the loss ratio of an IBM link ($\kappa/\gamma=4.2$) the best pulses found by optimal control lie within $4$–$12\%$ of it (Figure 1). Machine-checked in Isabelle (\texttt{loss\_floor}), for derivatives outside a countable set.

🛠️ How it is proved 3 / 5

What the paper contributes

A qubit hands its state to another qubit through a lossy bus (a resonator, a waveguide or a cable mode), with each coupler bounded by $G$. The paper gives error floors that hold for every control pulse, every duration and any number of bus modes, and applies them to IBM’s meter-scale interconnect.

  • A floor at every duration (Theorem 4.1, Isabelle): $P(T)\le e^{-\gamma T}/[1+(\kappa-\gamma)/(G^2T)]$.
  • The end qubits cannot be bypassed (Theorem 5.2, Isabelle): the excitation must sit in the end qubits for an integrated time whose lower bound tends to $\pi/(2G)$ for a complete transfer.
  • The asymptotic constant (Theorems 6.2 and 6.3): the perturbative optimum $2\sqrt{2\gamma(\kappa-\gamma)}/G$ becomes a lower bound over all controls, and an explicit pulse attains it with explicit error terms.
  • Dephasing and real devices (Section 7): the coherence with the vacuum is an amplitude with rates $2/T_2$; a version with a rate for each element gives the floor of a real device; reciprocity makes the direction irrelevant.
  • IBM’s links (Section 8): floors $6.03$–$10.05\times10^{-4}$ on $1-F_B$, certified with ball arithmetic, against measured errors $11$–$16$ times larger; which hardware change lowers the floor; three other platforms.

It rests on:

  • Quantum speed limits and optimal control — Cauchy–Schwarz on the integrated bus occupation; comparison with ODE windows; a calibration function Φ for the end qubits
  • Open quantum systems — no-jump evolution, Lindblad dephasing, coherence with the vacuum as an amplitude, reciprocity
  • Formal verification and certified numerics — Isabelle/HOL-Analysis (entry Quantum_Speed_Limits, public in karlesmarin/formalizations); ball arithmetic (Arb) for the IBM floors
