Lemma 21the real roots form a factor

Suppose \(f_u\) is reducible over \({\mathbb {Q}}(u)\) for every root \(u\) of \(Q_k\), and let \(y(u)\in (0,1)\) be the real root of \(f_u\). Then the set \(\{ y(u):Q_k(u)=0\} \) is stable under \(\operatorname {Gal}(\overline{\mathbb {Q}}/{\mathbb {Q}})\), and the primitive integral multiple \(h\) of \(\prod _u(Y-y(u))\) lies in \({\mathbb {Z}}[Y]\), has degree \(d\), divides \(P_k\), and has leading coefficient \(B\mid A\).

Lemma 14the nonabelian part cannot cancel

Let \(L/{\mathbb {Q}}\) be a finite Galois extension containing the roots of \(Q_k\) and of every \(f_{u_0}\) for \(u_0\) a root of \(Q_k\), and let \(G=\operatorname {Gal}(L/{\mathbb {Q}})\). Fix a \(G\)-orbit \(\mathcal O\) of roots of \(Q_k\), let \(H\) be the stabiliser of one of them, and let \(K=\ker \bigl(H\to S_3\bigr)\) for the action of \(H\) on the three roots of that fibre’s cubic. For \(p\) unramified in \(L\) and \(p\nmid 6k\mathfrak D_k\), let \(N_{\mathcal O}(p)\) be the number of roots of \(Q_k\) in \(\mathcal O\) that lie in \({\mathbb {F}}_p\) and whose cubic splits completely. Then

\[ { \boxed {\displaystyle |H/K|\; N_{\mathcal O}(p)\; =\; \chi _{E[G/K]}(\operatorname {Frob}_p)}} , \qquad \text{so}\qquad 6\, N_{\mathcal O}(p)=\frac{6}{|H/K|}\, \chi _{E[G/K]}(\operatorname {Frob}_p) , \]

with \(E\) a splitting field for \(G\) as in (5), the factor \(6/|H/K|\) being a positive rational. If moreover \(H/K\simeq S_3\) — which Proposition 13 provides for some orbit, and where the factor is \(1\) — then \(E[G/K]\) has an irreducible constituent of dimension greater than one. Since the orbits enter Theorem 12 with coefficient \(+1\) each, that constituent cannot cancel, and \(C_k(3,p)\) is not PORC.

Lemma 1the singular locus; the case \(k=3\) is Segre’s

Let \(\lambda =(w_1,\dots ,w_l)\) with \(w_i\) positive and \(\sum _iw_i=n\), and let \(V_\lambda \) be as in (1) over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\) or \(p{\gt}\max (n,k)\). A point \(z\in V_\lambda \) is singular if and only if, after scaling, \(z_i\in \mu _m\) for every \(i\) and \(\sum _iw_iz_i=0\). All such singularities are ordinary double points.

Observation 20the two failures are not the same failure; cf.  [ 17 ]

At \(n=3\), \(k=6\) the count takes exactly two polynomial values, \(p-1\) and \(0\), and the partition of the primes between them is a splitting condition in a nonabelian extension — the class function is the indicator of a conjugacy class, not a character. It is therefore not PORC but it is polynomial on Frobenius sets, POFS in the sense of Stanojkovski–Voll  [ 47 , § 1.5.2 ] . For \(n\ge 4\) the surviving object is the Frobenius trace of a curve of positive genus, and the count is not POFS either — not because of how many values it takes, but because a POFS function is Artin–Tate in the sense of (5) and Theorem 8 rules that out by weight one. The two failures are therefore separated by weight, and the numerical growth of the set of values is an illustration of the difference rather than the argument for it. The archetype usually offered for the first mode is du Sautoy–Vaughan-Lee  [ 5 ] , whose non-PORC behaviour is likewise a finite-valued count partitioned by a nonabelian splitting condition rather than a surviving trace; Isham’s separation  [ 17 , § 2.4.2 ] is the one that already contains both.

Proposition 5the converse fails at \(k=5\), \(n=6\)

Let \(k=5\), so \(m=4\), and \(\lambda =(3,1,1,1)\). Then \(V_\lambda \) is a geometrically irreducible plane quintic with exactly one node, its normalisation has genus \(5\), and consequently \(C_5(6,p)\) is not elementary — although \(\lambda =(3,1,1,1)\) and the other length-four partition \((2,2,1,1)\) of \(6\) are both singular, so that \(6\in S_4\).

Proposition 15the degree of \(Q_k\)

For every \(k\ge 3\),

\[ { \boxed {\displaystyle \deg Q_k=\Bigl\lfloor \frac k6\Bigr\rfloor -\bigl[\, k\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 6)\, \bigr]}} . \]

In particular \(\deg Q_k=0\) exactly for \(k\in \{ 3,4,5,7\} \), and since the degree is nondecreasing along each residue class modulo \(6\), no further \(k\) can return to zero.

Proposition 16the degree is a dimension of modular forms

Substitute \(e_2=-3E_4\) and \(e_3=2E_6\), with \(E_4,E_6\) the normalised Eisenstein series. Then \(\mathcal F_k=s_k(-3E_4,2E_6)\) is a modular form of weight \(2k\) for \(\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb {Z}})\), and since \(M_*(\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb {Z}}))=\mathbf C[E_4,E_6]\) a basis of \(M_{2k}\) is indexed by the solutions of \(2a+3b=k\) — the same set that indexes the terms of \(s_k\). Hence

\[ { \boxed {\displaystyle \dim M_{2k}=\deg Q_k+1}}, \]

and \(\dim M_{2k}=1\) exactly for \(k\in \{ 3,4,5,7\} \).

Remark 6the higher strata still contribute nothing of weight one, and why

For \(l\ge 5\) the variety \(V_\lambda \) has dimension \(d=l-3\ge 2\) and is cut in \(\mathbf P^{l-1}\) by \(r=2\) forms, so Ghorpade–Lachaud  [ 7 , Prop. 3.2 ] gives \(H^i(\mathbf P^{l-1})\to H^i(V_\lambda )\) an isomorphism for \(i\le l-1-2-1=l-4\); taking \(i=1\) needs exactly \(l\ge 5\), and yields \(H^1(V_\lambda )=0\). Blowing up the nodes, the exceptional fibres are smooth quadrics of dimension \(d-1\ge 1\), connected and with vanishing \(H^1\), so proper base change gives \(H^1(Y_\lambda )=0\) on the resolution as well; the quadrics contribute only even weights. What must be stressed is that this uses that the singularities are nodes, supplied by Lemma 1; it is not an assertion about singular hypersurfaces in general.

Remark 3the minimality hypothesis is where the work is

It is worth saying why the second case must be treated separately rather than absorbed into the first. Mann’s theorem bounds the ratios inside a minimal subsum, not between two of them, and four terms splitting as \(2+2\) can have ratios of arbitrarily large order:

\[ 1+(-1)+\zeta _7+(-\zeta _7)=0 \]

is a vanishing sum of four roots of unity with positive rational weights in which one ratio has order \(7\). A proof of Proposition 2 that decomposed into minimal subsums and then applied Mann to the whole relation would be answering a different question. For four terms Mann’s bound is already sharp — the relation \(2\cdot 1+(-1)+\omega +\omega ^{2}=0\) realises a ratio of order exactly \(6\) — and the sharper bound of Conway and Jones  [ 23 , Thm. 5 ] , which requires \(l\ge \sum _{q\mid r}(q-2)+2\), agrees with it here and only improves on it from five terms onwards.

Remark 7“no weight one” does not mean “Artin–Tate”

At the same parameters \(k=5\), \(n=6\), take \(\lambda =(2,1,1,1,1)\). Then \(V_\lambda \) is a nodal quintic surface in \(\mathbf P^3\); its dualising sheaf is \(\mathcal O(1)\) and resolving ordinary double points preserves the geometric genus, so the resolution has \(h^{2,0}=4\). Its \(H^2\) is genuinely not Artin–Tate, even though \(H^1\) vanishes. The weight-one argument is therefore an obstruction and not a converse theorem: it can prove that a count fails to be elementary, never that it succeeds. Every positive statement below is proved by exhibiting the formula.

Remark 10the case \(k=4\) has a name, and is classified

For \(n=3\) and \(k=4\), Newton under \(e_1=0\) gives \(s_4=2e_2^2\), so the condition \(s_1=s_4=0\) is \(s_1=s_2=0\); a triple of distinct elements with \(s_1=s_2=0\) and \(s_3\ne 0\) is precisely a super-Vandermonde set of size three in the sense of Sziklai–Takáts  [ 45 ] , whose Theorem 1.12 shows that such a set of size \(t{\lt}p\) is a coset of a multiplicative subgroup — for \(t=3\), exactly the sets \(a\{ 1,\omega ,\omega ^2\} \) that the proof above produces. Our first formula therefore recovers a classification that has been available since 2008, by a different route; we claim nothing for it, and Blokhuis et al.  [ 22 ] is the current state of that line. What is not in that literature is our actual object: the invariant \(w_T\) there is by construction the first non-vanishing moment, so the prescribed condition is always an initial segment \(s_1=\dots =s_{t-1}=0\), whereas we prescribe \(s_1=s_k=0\) and leave everything between them free. Already at \(p=19\) and \(k=6\), all eighteen triples with \(s_1=s_6=0\) have \(s_2\ne 0\) and are therefore not Vandermonde sets of any kind.

Remark 18what the column is really counting

Normalise the cubic attached to a root \(u\) of \(Q_k\) as \(f_u(Y)=Y^3+uY-u\), with \(\operatorname {disc}(f_u)=-u^{2}(4u+27)\), and let \(E_u\) be the elliptic curve \(V^2=f_u(Y)\), of \(j\)-invariant \(6912u/(4u+27)\). Then

\[ { \boxed {\displaystyle f_u\ \text{splits completely over }{\mathbb {F}}_p \iff E_u[2]\subseteq E_u({\mathbb {F}}_p)}} , \]

because \(S_3=\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb {F}}_2)\) acting on the three nonzero \(2\)-torsion points is exactly the Galois action on the roots. So at \(n=3\) the count is of fibres with fully rational \(2\)-torsion, over a finite set of values of the modular parameter. This resolves what looks like a paradox in Observation 20: there are elliptic curves behind the zero-dimensional problem too, but what is counted there is their torsion, which takes finitely many states, and not their trace.

Remark 19a null that a short window would have manufactured

For \(k=9\) one has \(s_9=-3e_3(3e_2^3-e_3^2)\), so the count should take two values. Over the \(14\) primes \(5\le p\le 53\) it takes one: the second family first appears at \(p=71\), and then at \(83,97,131,193,257\). A family of Chebotarev density \(1/6\) is expected about twice in fourteen primes, so its absence there is not evidence of absence. We record this because the criterion is general: a null result on a window too short to see the predicted density is not a null result.
