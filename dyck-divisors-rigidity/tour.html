🎯 The result 1 / 5

Proposition 3.1.

  1. Let \(c\in [-b-1,b]^{2a+1}\) (realisable or not), \(|h|\le a\) and \(c_h\le b-1\). Then

    \[ \Sigma (c+e_h)-\Sigma (c)=(a+1-|h|)(b+1-|c_h+1|). \]

  2. Crossing the wall \(\ell _{h,\rho }:\Lambda +h\theta =\rho \) (\(|\rho |\le b\)) at a point on no other line, from \(\Lambda +h\theta {\lt}\rho \) to \(\Lambda +h\theta {\gt}\rho \), changes only \(c_h\), from \(\rho -1\) to \(\rho \), and raises \(\Sigma \) by exactly \(\omega (h,\rho )\ge 1\).

  3. For realisable \(c,d\), \(\Sigma (d)-\Sigma (c)=\sum _{|h|\le a}(a+1-|h|)\bigl(F(d_h)-F(c_h)\bigr)\), where \(F(v)=\sum _{j=0}^{b}f(v+j)\) and \(F(v)-F(u)=\sum _{\rho =u+1}^{v}(b+1-|\rho |)\) for \(u\le v\): the signed sum of the weights of the walls that separate the two cells, independent of the path.

💬 In plain words 2 / 5

Add up all the numbers of the multiset $Z(c)$ that encodes the word: that is $§igma(c)$. Each coordinate $c_h$ enters $a+1-|h|$ rows, so moving across one line $\Lambda+h\theta=\rho$ of the arrangement (the only coordinate that changes is $c_h$, from $\rho-1$ to $\rho$) raises $§igma$ by the explicit positive number $\omega(h,\rho)=(a+1-|h|)(b+1-|\rho|)$. So $§igma$ is a “height” that strictly increases upwards through every wall.

Why it matters: two cells next to each other differ in height, so they never have the same word; two comparable vectors $c\le d$, $c\ne d$, differ in height too, so every fibre (the set of cells with one word) is an antichain; and a coincidence must cross walls of equal total weight upwards and downwards. Combined with a decomposition into vertical strips (Proposition 3.5), this bounds every fibre by $4ab+1$ cells. The paper calls these results elementary and claims no novelty for them; parts (a), (c) and the positivity of the weight are checked in Isabelle/HOL. The lab draws every cell as a terrace at height $§igma(c)$, as in the paper's Figure 2.

🛠️ How it is proved 3 / 5

What the paper contributes

The language of the words $\langle\!\langle n\rangle\!\rangle_2$ and its questions are Rodríguez Caballero's; paper I (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24541288) counted the cells $R(a,b)$ of the pattern $r^as^b$ and asked whether the excess $E(a,b)=R(a,b)-W(a,b)$ is linear in $b$ for $a\ge2$ (rigidity). This draft answers it for $a\le5$ and studies where the coincidences live, at three levels of status:

  • Level 1, general theory (every $a$; written proofs, re-derived by an independent AI agent, not checked by a human referee). A dimension criterion (Proposition 4.1) and a finite reduction to affine observation vectors on pieces of the parameter space (Theorem 5.1). Crossing the wall $\Lambda+h\theta=\rho$ raises $\Sigma(c)$ by exactly $\omega(h,\rho)=(a+1-|h|)(b+1-|\rho|)$ (Proposition 3.1), so adjacent cells have different words, every fibre is an antichain of at most $4ab+1$ cells, $E\le\mathrm{Amb}\le2E$, and boundedly many power sums of $Z(c)$ determine the word. From Alekseyev's count, $R(a,b)=C_ab(b+1)+(a+1)b+\pi_a(b)$ with $\pi_a$ of period dividing $P_a$ (Proposition 2.3; the count itself is classical).
  • Level 2, computer-assisted rigidity (program and reduction not checked by a human referee). $E(a,b)=\Theta(b)$ and $W(a,b)=C_ab^2+O(b)$ for $a=2,3,4,5$ (Theorem 6.1); the only affine candidates shift $n$ by a non-integral multiple of $1/(a+1)$, explained by the smallest element $\tau(c)$ of $Z(c)$ (Proposition 7.1). For every $a\ge2$ the all-positive chamber has no two-parameter family (Theorems 7.6 and 7.10, Corollary 7.11, written proofs; Theorem 7.10 and Proposition 7.4 are checked in Isabelle/HOL). Measured by length, ambiguity does not vanish: lower bounds on both sides of $\theta=1/(a+1)$ give a mean ambiguous share with $\liminf\ge3/(4a(a+1)|I|)$ (Proposition 6.3, Corollary 6.4). Lemma D for $a=3$, $b\ge317$ (Theorem 8.1).
  • Level 3, a conjectural classification for $a=3$. Nine one-parameter families of coincidences, each proved by hand (Theorems 9.2, 9.4, Proposition 9.1, Corollary 9.5), give $E(3,b)$ up to a periodic constant fitted to the data (Conjecture 9.8): it agrees with exact enumeration for $5\le b\le170$ and some $b\le400$; completeness is not proved.

Fifteen written results are checked, wholly or in part, in Isabelle/HOL (public: karlesmarin/formalizations, Isabelle/CaballeroDyckWords). What the paper claims as its own, as far as its searches could see: the finite reduction and its certificate, Lemma D, the all-positive-chamber analysis and the families of Section 9; the rest is known, standard or elementary and is labelled so in the paper. Draft of 26 November 2026, not refereed, not published.

It rests on:

  • Paper I of the series (Rodríguez Caballero’s language L) — the cells of the lines Λ + hθ = ρ, the multiset Z(c) that encodes the word, realisation by primes, the count R(a,b), Woods’ quasipolynomiality, W(1,b) and W(2,b), the two tools of its Supplement (Lemma M and Lemma P)
  • Threshold functions (Acketa–Žunić 1991; Alekseyev 2010) — R(a,b) = (N(2b,2a) + 4a + 4b + 6)/8 through Alekseyev’s exact count; the linear term D_a = C_a + a + 1 and the period bound P_a are read off it by Möbius inversion
  • Semilinear sets (Ginsburg–Spanier) and polyhedra (Hoffman; Minkowski–Weyl) — the dimension criterion for E(a,b) = O(b); the error bound in case (iv) of the finite reduction; the recession argument of Lemma S
  • Sturmian words (Mignosi; Berstel–Pocchiola; Lothaire) — the templates ε are the factors of length 2a of mechanical words, counted by Mignosi’s formula; the non-integrality theorem is a statement on equal window sums of two such factors
  • Sparse polynomials (Hajós’s lemma) — finitely many power sums of Z(c), a number depending on a only, determine the word
