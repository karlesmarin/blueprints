Every 4×4 biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of eleven squares

🏷️ Real algebraic geometry · sums of squares on projective varieties

Carles Marín

At a glance

The exact value BSR(4,4) = 11: every biquadratic sum of squares in x1..4, y1..4 is a sum of eleven squares of bilinear forms. Qi and Cui gave a form that needs eleven; the previous upper bound was twelve. The proof classifies the Hankel multipliers of rank at most five whose image contains no product (Lemmas 2 and 3) and rules out the two shapes left with second variations built from the alternating forms (Propositions 6 and 8).

📐 Setting (copied from the paper, §§1–2)

Write \(x=(x_1,\dots ,x_4)\), \(y=(y_1,\dots ,y_4)\), \(V=\mathbb R^4\otimes \mathbb R^4\) (the bilinear forms) and \(R_2\) the biquadratic forms, of dimension \(100\). \(\mathrm{BSR}(m,n)\) is the least \(r\) such that every biquadratic sum of squares in \(m+n\) variables is a sum of \(r\) squares of bilinear forms; \(\Phi (h_1,\dots ,h_{11})=h_1^2+\dots +h_{11}^2\), \(\Sigma _{11}=\Phi (V^{11})\) and \(\Sigma \) the cone of sums of squares. A multiplier is a symmetric bilinear form \(H\) on \(A\otimes B\) with the Hankel symmetry \(H(x\otimes b,y\otimes c)=H(x\otimes c,y\otimes b)\); its supports \(a,b_0\) are the dimensions of the two factors modulo the radicals. Writing \(H=T^*JT\) with \(T\) onto \(E\), \(\dim E=\operatorname {rk}H\), and \(T_bx=T(x\otimes b)\), the symmetry reads \(\langle T_bx,T_cy\rangle _J=\langle T_cx,T_by\rangle _J\).

↳ Not used by another result of the paper.

Take a biquadratic form in \(x_1,\dots ,x_4\) and \(y_1,\dots ,y_4\) that is a sum of squares of bilinear forms, however many. The theorem says that eleven squares always suffice, and Qi and Cui gave a form for which ten do not. Before this note the best upper bound was twelve, from width three. The proof does not search for representations: it shows that the set of sums of eleven squares has no boundary inside the cone, because any boundary point would carry a Hankel multiplier of rank at most five that the classification of Lemmas 2–3 and the second variations of Propositions 6 and 8 rule out.

Proof ▼

The three cases below are exhaustive: \(s=4\) with some \(Q(b)\) invertible, \(s=4\) with every \(Q(b)\) singular, and \(s\le 3\).

(i) Some \(Q(b)\) is invertible. Choosing bases by congruence, \(E={\mathbb {C}}^4\oplus {\mathbb {C}}\) with the standard symmetric product and \(T_b x=(x,0)\). In a basis of a complement of \(b\) write \(T_i x=(A_ix,\, u_i^\top x)\), \(i=1,2,3\). By (2),

\begin{equation} A_i^\top =A_i,\qquad [A_i,A_j]=u_ju_i^\top -u_iu_j^\top . \end{equation}
1

With \(v\odot w=vw^\top +wv^\top \) and \(K_{ij}=A_iu_j-A_ju_i\), the Jacobi identity for \(A_1,A_2,A_3\) and (4) give

\begin{equation} K_{12}\odot u_3+K_{23}\odot u_1+K_{31}\odot u_2=0 . \end{equation}
2

If the \(u_i\) span \(0\), \(T\) is not onto. If they span a line, the last coordinate of \(T\) is a simple covector of \(\operatorname{im}H\). If they span a plane, after a change of basis of the complement \(u_3=0\) with \(u_1,u_2\) independent; then \(A_3\) commutes with \(A_1,A_2\), and (5) gives \((A_3u_1)\odot u_2=(A_3u_2)\odot u_1\), whence \(A_3u_1=tu_1\), \(A_3u_2=tu_2\) for one \(t\) (compare coefficients in a basis starting with \(u_1,u_2\)). The space \(P=\operatorname{im}(A_3-tI)\) is non-zero (otherwise \(T_3=tT_b\) and \(B\) would have a radical, against \(b_0=4\)), stable under all \(A_i\), and orthogonal to \(u_1,u_2\) since \(A_3\) is symmetric; by (4) the \(A_i\) commute on \(P\), and a common eigenvector \(x\in P\) has \(T_cx\in {\mathbb {C}}(x,0)\) for all \(c\), so the Observation applies. If the \(u_i\) are independent, (5) forces \(K_{ij}=t_iu_j-t_ju_i\) for three scalars \(t_i\) (complete \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) to a basis: the terms with the fourth vector and the squares \(u_i^{2}\) force \(K_{12}\in \langle u_1,u_2\rangle \), \(K_{23}\in \langle u_2,u_3\rangle \), \(K_{31}\in \langle u_3,u_1\rangle \), and the coefficients of \(u_1u_2\), \(u_2u_3\), \(u_3u_1\) give the signs). Then the symmetric matrices

\[ \widehat A_i=\begin{pmatrix} A_i & u_i \\ u_i^\top & t_i \end{pmatrix} \]

commute (the upper-left block of their commutator vanishes by (4), the upper right one is \(K_{ij}+t_ju_i-t_iu_j=0\), and the rest follows by antisymmetry). A common eigenvector \(z\ne 0\) exists over \({\mathbb {C}}\); if \(\widehat A_iz=\lambda _iz\), then \(\langle z,T_ix\rangle =\lambda _i\langle z,T_bx\rangle \), since \(T_ix=\widehat A_iT_bx\), so \(T^*Jz\) is simple, and non-zero because \(T\) is onto.

(ii) Some \(T_b\) has rank \(4\) and every \(Q(b)\) is singular. Then \(U=\operatorname{im}T_b\) is a degenerate hyperplane with a radical of dimension one. In a Witt basis \(E={\mathbb {C}}^3\oplus \langle e,f\rangle \), \(\langle e,f\rangle =1\), \(\langle e,e\rangle =\langle f,f\rangle =0\), with \(T_b(w,z)=(w,z,0)\), condition (2) gives each \(T_c\) the shape \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & p \\ l^\top & t \\ p^\top & q_0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(A=A^\top \). In \(Q(b+\tau c)\) the upper \(3\times 3\) block is \(I+O(\tau )\), the upper right block is \(2\tau p+O(\tau ^2)\) and the lower right entry is \(2\tau q_0+\tau ^2(p^\top p+2tq_0)\). Since the determinant vanishes identically, so does the Schur complement as a formal series; its terms of order one and two give \(q_0=0\) and \(-3p^\top p=0\). Thus \(p(c)^\top p(c)=0\) for every \(c\), and \(c\mapsto p(c)\) is linear; by polarization \(p(c)^\top p(c')=0\) for all \(c,c'\), so \(\{ p(c)\} \) spans a totally isotropic subspace of \({\mathbb {C}}^3\), of dimension at most one: \(p(c)=\beta (c)p_0\). The covector \(T^*Je\), \((w,z;c)\mapsto p(c)^\top w=\beta (c)\, p_0^\top w\), is simple and non-zero.

(iii) \(s\le 3\). By (3) and \(Q(b)\ne 0\) generically, \(s=3\) and \(\operatorname{rk}Q(b)=1\) generically: \(U\) has a radical of dimension at least two and \(Q(b)\neq 0\). So \(q(x,b)=x^\top Q(b)x\) is, over \({\mathbb {C}}(b)\), a scalar times the square of a linear form in \(x\) (complete the square from a non-zero diagonal entry). By Gauss’ lemma \(q=g(b)L(x,b)^2\) in \({\mathbb {C}}[b][x]\) with \(L\) primitive, and the factors of a bihomogeneous polynomial are bihomogeneous. If \(L\) has degree \(0\) in \(b\), the support in \(A\) is \(1\). Otherwise \(g\) is constant and \(L=x^\top Pb\), with \(P\) invertible by full support, so after changes of basis and scale \(q=(x^\top b)^2\). Its polar multiplier acts on matrices as \(X\mapsto (\operatorname{tr}(X)I+X^\top )/2\), which is invertible (\(X^\top =-\operatorname{tr}(X)I\) gives \(5\operatorname{tr}X=0\) and \(X=0\)): rank \(16\ne 5\).

Proof ▼

Restrict \(H\) to \(A'\otimes B'\), with \(A',B'\) complements of the two radicals: the rank, the symmetry (1) and the absence of simple tensors are kept (a simple covector of the restricted image extends by zero on the radicals and stays simple and in \(\operatorname{im}H\)), and now both radicals are zero, with \(\dim A'=a\), \(\dim B'=b_0\). Since \(\operatorname{im}H\) annihilates the radicals, \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{im}H)\) is a subspace of dimension \(r-1\) avoiding the Segre variety of the effective factors, so \(r\le (a-1)(b_0-1)\); and \(a,b_0\ge 2\) since \(H\ne 0\).

We show \(r\ge a+1\), and by the symmetry of (1) in the two factors also \(r\ge b_0+1\). Suppose \(r\le a\) (\(\le 4\)) and take \(b\) generic, with \(s=\operatorname{rk}T_b\) maximal and \(Q(b)\ne 0\). If \(s{\lt}a\), then \(\ker T_b\ne 0\), and the radical of \(U=\operatorname{im}T_b\) has dimension at most \(s-1\) (as \(Q(b)\ne 0\)) and at most \(r-s\) (as it lies in \(U^{\perp _J}\)), so at most \(\min (s-1,r-s)\le 1\) because \(r\le 4\); this contradicts (3) and the Observation. If \(s=a\), then \(s=a=r\), \(T_b:A'\to E\) is an isomorphism, \(Q(b)\) is non-degenerate, and the operators \(T_b^{-1}T_c\) are self-adjoint for \(Q(b)\) and, by (2), commute; a common eigenvector \(x\) has \(T_cx\in {\mathbb {C}}\, T_bx\) for all \(c\), and the Observation gives a simple tensor. So \(r\ge \max (a,b_0)+1\).

With \(r\le 5\) and \(r\le (a-1)(b_0-1)\) the only possibilities are \((a,b_0,r)=(4,4,5)\), \((4,3,5)\), \((3,4,5)\) and \((3,3,4)\), and Lemma 2 excludes \((4,4,5)\).

Proof ▼

If \(S\subseteq \ker H\), then \(H(s,v)=H(v,s)=0\) for all \(s\in S\) and \(v\in V\), and \(S\) is the space of symmetric tensors, so \(H(x\otimes y,x\otimes y)=-H(x\otimes y,y\otimes x)=-H(x\otimes x,y\otimes y)=0\) for all \(x,y\), and \(H=0\) by polarization. Hence the products of the ten summands with \(V\) span \(R_2\) (we also checked the rank \(100\) exactly), and adding an eleventh summand, even zero, gives a point where \(d\Phi \) is onto.

If \(\Sigma _{11}\neq \Sigma \), there are \(F\in \operatorname {int}\Sigma \cap \partial \Sigma _{11}\), a representation \(F=\sum _{i=1}^{11}h_i^2\) and an indefinite real multiplier \(H\) on \(V\) such that, with \(W=\operatorname {span}(h_i)\): \(W\subseteq \ker H\), \(\dim W=11\) and hence \(\operatorname{rk}H\le 5\); the complex image of \(H\) contains no simple tensor; and \(\sum _ih_iD_i=0\) in \(R_2\) implies \(\sum _iH(D_i,D_i)\ge 0\).

🆕 Classical and new: the paper: the argument of [Mar, Proposition 3 and Corollary 4], after [BDS]; only the jet count 100 − 14 + 12 = 98 < 99 depends on the size.

↳ Used in: Theorem 1, Proposition 6, Proposition 8

Proof ▼

We adapt the argument of [ 3 , Proposition 3 and Corollary 4 ] to \((m,n)=(4,4)\), using Lemma 4. The only count that depends on the size is the one excluding forms with a singular point with no real factor. Take a pair of conjugate points distinct in both factors and change coordinates over \({\mathbb {C}}\) to \((e_1,e_1),(e_2,e_2)\). A first jet has seven coordinates, the value and the six tangent derivatives, and the two first jets use disjoint sets of seven monomial coefficients, so the joint jet map is surjective over \({\mathbb {C}}\). Conjugation exchanges the two jets, and the restriction to real forms has real rank fourteen. The point varies in real dimension twelve. The incidence therefore has dimension at most \(100-14+12=98\), and its projection cannot fill the boundary patch of dimension \(99\). A singular point with a real factor is impossible at an interior form, by definiteness of the quadratic form in the other factor. Independence of the \(h_i\) is as in [ 3 , Corollary 4 ] .

Proposition 6
#

  • Lemma 2

📕 p. 4📍 map❝ cite⚠ report

No triple \((F,h,H)\) satisfying the conclusions of Proposition 5 can have \((a,b_0,r)=(3,4,5)\) or \((4,3,5)\).

↳ Used in: Theorem 1, 7

Proof ▼

Here \(\dim \ker H=16-5=11\), so \(W=\ker H\). Let \(x_0\) span the radical in the first factor and \(A'\) be a real complement, \(\dim A'=3\). Then \(x_0\otimes B\subseteq W\), and an orthogonal change of the eleven summands (an orthonormal basis of the preimage of \(x_0\otimes B\), completed) gives \(h_i=x_0\otimes y_i\), \(1\le i\le 4\), for a real basis \((y_i)\) of \(B\). For \(z_{ij}=-z_{ji}\in A'\) put

\[ D_i=\sum _{j=1}^4 z_{ij}\otimes y_j\quad (i\le 4),\qquad D_i=0\quad (i{\gt}4). \]

Then \(\sum _ih_iD_i=\sum _{i,j}x_0z_{ij}\, y_iy_j=0\) by antisymmetry: these are syzygies, a space \(A'\otimes \Lambda ^2B\) of dimension \(18\). With \(H=T^*JT\) as in §2 (now over \({\mathbb {R}}\), \(\dim E=5\)), the second variation is \(\sum _iH(D_i,D_i)=J^{\oplus 4}(\Psi z)\) for

\[ \Psi :A'\otimes \Lambda ^2B\to E^4,\qquad \Psi (z)=\Big(\sum _jT_{y_j}z_{ij}\Big)_{i=1}^4 . \]

By Proposition 5, \(J^{\oplus 4}\) is non-negative on \(\operatorname{im}\Psi \). Since \(H\) is indefinite, the positive index \(p\) of \(J\) is at most \(4\), and a subspace on which \(J^{\oplus 4}\) is non-negative has dimension at most \(4p\le 16\). Hence \(\operatorname{rk}\Psi \le 16\).

On the other hand \(\operatorname{rk}\Psi =17\). Complexify and compute the annihilator of \(\operatorname{im}\Psi \) under \(J^{\oplus 4}\): a tuple \((c_i)\in E^4\), i.e. a map \(C:B\to E\) with \(Cy_i=c_i\), annihilates \(\operatorname{im}\Psi \) if and only if the matrix \(\big(\langle Cy_i,T(a\otimes y_j)\rangle _J\big)_{ij}\) is symmetric for every \(a\in A'\) (vary each \(z_{ij}\), \(i{\lt}j\), separately). The three maps \(C=T(a\otimes \cdot )\), \(a\) in a basis of \(A'\), are independent and lie in the annihilator by (2). If there were a fourth independent \(C\), it would extend \(T\) to \(\widetilde T:{\mathbb {C}}^4\otimes B\to E\) satisfying (2): the identities involving the new row are exactly the symmetry just written, and the one of the new row with itself is automatic. Then \(\widetilde T^*J\widetilde T\) is a multiplier of rank \(5\) (as \(T\) is onto) with supports \(4\) and \(4\) (by independence of the new row, and since the second support was already full), and its image contains no simple tensor: a simple covector in it restricts to \(A'\otimes B\) as a simple covector of \(\operatorname{im}H\), excluded by Proposition 5, or as zero, and then its representing vector \(z\) has \(\langle z,T(A'\otimes B)\rangle _J=0\), so \(z=0\). This contradicts Lemma 2. So the annihilator has dimension \(3\) and \(\operatorname{rk}\Psi =20-3=17{\gt}16\), a contradiction.

Proof ▼

Let \(x_0,y_0\) span the real radicals of the two factors, \(A',B'\) complements and \(u=x_0\otimes y_0\). The space \(U=x_0\otimes B+A\otimes y_0\) has dimension \(7\) and lies in \(K=\ker H\), \(\dim K=12\); \(W\) is a hyperplane of \(K\), and \(K=U\oplus K_0\) with \(K_0=K\cap (A'\otimes B')\).

Case \(u\notin W\). Then \(W\cap (x_0\otimes B)=x_0\otimes L\) with \(\dim L=3\) and \(L\) projecting isomorphically onto \(B/\langle y_0\rangle \). Orthogonalizing on the preimage of this intersection, \(h_i=x_0\otimes y_i\), \(i\le 3\), for a basis of \(L\). The restriction of \(H\) to \(A'\otimes L\) keeps the indices, the rank four and the support three in the first factor. For \(v_1,v_2,v_3\in A'\) put \(D_i=\sum _{j,p}\varepsilon _{ijp}\, v_p\otimes y_j\) for \(i\le 3\) and \(D_i=0\) otherwise; they are syzygies by antisymmetry, and with \(H_{jl}\) the blocks of the restriction and \(S=\sum _jH_{jj}\), Proposition 5 and the contraction of the alternating symbols give, as in [ 3 , § 5 ] ,

\[ I_3\otimes S-H|_{A'\otimes L}\succeq 0 . \]

The diagonal blocks sum to \(2S\), so \(S\succeq 0\); if \(Sx=0\), the three diagonal values of this positive semidefinite form at \(x\otimes y_j\) sum to zero, so each \(x\otimes y_j\) is in its kernel, \(H(x\otimes y_j,\cdot )=0\), and \(x=0\) by full support in \(A'\). So \(S\succ 0\), and after the congruence by \(S^{-1/2}\) in the three blocks the matrix of \(H\) has trace \(3\), all eigenvalues at most \(1\), rank four and at least one negative eigenvalue: its trace is less than \(3\), a contradiction.

Case \(u\in W\). An orthogonal change makes \(h_1\) a non-zero multiple of \(u\). Let \(\ell \) be a real linear functional on \(K\) with \(W=\ker \ell \). For \(v=\sum v_{ij}a_i\otimes b_j\in A'\otimes B'\) (bases \(a_i\), \(b_j\)) the lift \(P(v)=\sum _{ij}v_{ij}(a_i\otimes y_0)(x_0\otimes b_j)\in \operatorname {Sym}^2U\) has product \(uv\) in \(R_2\). If \(\ell |_U=0\) all these products lie in \(WK\); otherwise \(\operatorname {Sym}^2U/(U\cap W)U\) is one-dimensional and the class is measured by \((\ell |_U)^2\). So \(uv\in WK\) whenever

\[ \phi (v)=\sum _{ij}v_{ij}\, \ell (a_i\otimes y_0)\, \ell (x_0\otimes b_j)=0 . \]

The functional \(\phi \) on the active block is zero or simple. If it is non-zero it does not vanish on \(K_0\), since otherwise it would lie in the image of the active part of \(H\), which contains no simple tensor. Choose \(v\) in the active block with \(H(v,v){\lt}0\); adding a real element of \(K_0\) keeps this value and achieves \(\phi (v)=0\). Then \(h_1v\in WK\): write \(-h_1v=\sum _ih_ik_i\) with \(k_i\in K\). The \(D_i=\delta _{i1}v+k_i\) form a syzygy with second variation \(H(v,v){\lt}0\), a contradiction.
