Definition copied from the note (Section ``A reformulation through the Kassel--Reutenauer  polynomials''): from \<open>P\<^sub>n(q)/q\<^sup>n\<^sup>-\<^sup>1 = \<Sum> (-1)\<^sup>k\<^sup>-\<^sup>1 [[-(m'-k-1)/2, (m'-k-1)/2]]\<close> over the pairs  \<open>1 \<le> k < m'\<close>, \<open>k m' = 2n\<close>, \<open>k \<not>\<equiv> m' (mod 2)\<close>, coefficients of \<open>q\<^sup>i\<close> give  \<open>a\<^sub>{n,i} = \<Sum>\<^sub>j \<epsilon>\<^sub>j [d\<^sub>j \<ge> 2i+1]\<close>, where \<open>k\<^sub>1 < \<dots> < k\<^sub>K\<close> are the smaller members of the pairs,  \<open>d\<^sub>j = 2n/k\<^sub>j - k\<^sub>j\<close> and \<open>\<epsilon>\<^sub>j = (-1)\<^sup>k\<^sub>j\<^sup>-\<^sup>1\<close>. We start from that coefficient formula.   Proved: the \<open>d\<^sub>j\<close> are odd and satisfy \<open>d\<^sub>j \<ge> d\<^sub>j\<^sub>+\<^sub>1 + 2\<close>; \<open>a\<^sub>{n,i} = h\<^sub>{J(i)}\<close> with \<open>h\<^sub>j\<close> the partial  sums of the \<open>\<epsilon>\<close>; \<open>J(0) = K\<close>, \<open>J(i) \<ge> 1\<close> for \<open>i \<le> n-1\<close>; \<open>J\<close> is antitone; every value \<open>j \<in> [1,K]\<close>  is attained at some \<open>i \<le> n-1\<close> (the runs are nonempty).   \<^bold>\<open>Not proved here:\<close> (a) that the \<open>\<epsilon>\<^sub>j\<close> are the first symmetry of the  height sequence, which give the second half of the coefficients; (c) the Kassel--Reutenauer identity  itself, which is taken from the source and not rederived.\<close> subsection
