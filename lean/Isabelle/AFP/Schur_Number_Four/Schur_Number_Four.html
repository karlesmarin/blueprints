Baumert found by computer that the largest \<open>n\<close> with a Schur-free 4-colouring is 44  (S. W. Golomb and L. D. Baumert, Backtrack Programming, J. ACM 12 (1965), 516--524).   Upper bound: colour swaps, proved in HOL, take any Schur-free 4-colouring of \<open>{1..45}\<close> to one whose colours  appear for the first time in the order 0, 1, 2, 3: \<open>f 1 = 0\<close>, \<open>f 2 = 1\<close> and \<open>f k = 2\<close> for the first \<open>k\<close>  with another colour, \<open>k \<in> {3, 4, 5}\<close> (the case where \<open>{1..5}\<close> uses two colours is a fourth, tiny case).  Each case is encoded by explicit instances of three facts about the  colouring (each colour is one of \<open>0..3\<close>, at most one colour per number, no monochromatic triple), refuted by a  SAT solver, and the LRAT proof is replayed by the kernel (method \<open>lrat\<close>).
