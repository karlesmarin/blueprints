As an analogue of the four-vertex theorem, Tabachnikov  \cite{Tabachnikov2018} asked whether the differences of the entries of a fixed row of two frieze patterns  of the same width must change sign at least four times along the period. He proved it for the first two  rows and in an infinitesimal version, reported a counterexample of M. Cuntz with positive rational entries  (width $5$, middle row), and left open whether the statement holds for frieze patterns of positive  integers.   This entry shows that it fails for integers too, in the strongest form: for every even $n \geq 14$ there  are two integer frieze patterns of width $n - 3$ whose middle rows are strictly ordered at every position,  so that their difference does not change sign at all. Rows are numbered as in \cite{Tabachnikov2018}, and  the middle row is row $\lceil w/2 \rceil$ of a frieze of width $w$. (The range  $1 \leq k \leq \lfloor w/2 \rfloor$ printed there would leave out the middle row of odd width, but the  example of Cuntz given in the same paper is a middle row as well.)   The construction starts from a pair for $n = 14$ and, at each step, inserts two ears at antipodal positions  of both triangulations. Frieze patterns are represented by unimodular lattice polygons, a standard  description \cite{MorierGenoud2015}; in this representation, inserting an ear and computing the new  diameter are consequences of the bilinearity of the determinant. Section 4 connects the representation  with the usual one: every entry is a continuant of the quiddity.
