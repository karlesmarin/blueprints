A *monotone invariant* (`MonotoneInvariant`) is a function `μ` from square matrices over a ring `P`to `ℕ∞` that is unchanged by relabelling, by padding with an identity block and by multiplicationby invertible matrices, and that does not decrease when passing from `F` to a product `G₁ F G₂`.(In the paper square matrices are indexed by `1, …, n`, so relabelling is invisible there; herematrices are indexed by arbitrary finite types and invariance under `reindex` is part of thedefinition.) Stable association (`StablyAssociated`, Cohn §0.5) is association after padding withidentity blocks. ## What is a hypothesis The ring-theoretic input of Theorem 3.4 is **not** formalized here: * the matrix localization lemma (Lemma 3.1), which rests on the theory of firs and  semifirs (Cohn 2006, §3.2, Thm 3.2.3, Prop. 7.5.9; Schofield 2007, Thm 5.6);* the existence of a complete factorization `Q = G₁ 𝒜 G₂` with an atom `𝒜` stably associated with  the given atomic factor, and the transitivity of stable association (Cohn 2006, Cor. 0.5.5). `not_inverseInU` takes their combined conclusion as the named hypothesis `hloc`: if `H⁻¹` hasentries in `U_Σ` (an opaque proposition `inverseInU`), then some `Q ∈ Σ` factors as `G₁ 𝒜 G₂` with`𝒜` stably associated with the atomic factor `G` of `H`. Neither atoms nor `U_Σ` appear otherwise:the argument only uses the two conditions of Definition 3.3. ## Main results * `MonotoneInvariant.eq_of_stablyAssoc`: a monotone invariant is constant on stable  association classes.* `MonotoneInvariant.le_of_factor`: if `Q = G₁ 𝒜 G₂` with `𝒜` stably associated with `G`, then  `μ G ≤ μ Q`.* `not_inverseInU` (Theorem 3.4): if every `Q ∈ Σ` has a monotone invariant `μ_Q` with  `μ_Q Q < μ_Q G`, then `H⁻¹` does not have entries in `U_Σ` (given `hloc`).* `corankInvariant` (Example (b)): for a ring homomorphism `Θ : P →+* Matrix (Fin m) (Fin m) K`  into matrices over a field, the corank of the evaluated matrix `F^Θ` is a monotone invariant.  The monotonicity is Mathlib's `Matrix.rank_mul_le_left` / `Matrix.rank_mul_le_right`; invariance  under invertible factors is `Matrix.rank_mul_eq_left_of_isUnit_det` /  `Matrix.rank_mul_eq_right_of_isUnit_det`; padding uses the block bounds of  `Formalizations.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.Rank`. Here `K` is commutative (Mathlib's `Matrix.rank`  needs a commutative ring); the paper allows a skew field. ## References * P. M.
