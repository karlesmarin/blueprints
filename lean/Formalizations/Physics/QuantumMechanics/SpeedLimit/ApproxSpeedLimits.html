Here the target is reached only up to a **state infidelity** `ε`. Everythingfollows from the triangle inequality for the Fubini–Study angle`fsAngle u v = arccos ‖⟪u, v⟫‖`: a state of fidelity `≥ 1 − ε` with a target lies at angle`≤ arccos √(1 − ε) = arcsin √ε` from it. Here `ε` is the infidelity of one specific input/output pair of states, `‖⟪target, U ψ⟫‖² ≥ 1 − ε`.It is **not** an average gate infidelity (which would need an average over inputs). The statements were suggested by an external review. ## Main results * `QSL.Qudit.pi_div_four_sub_le_entangling_time_approx`: if the evolution maps a unit product  vector to `Φ` with `‖⟪Bell, Φ⟫‖² ≥ 1 − ε`, then `π/4 − arcsin √ε ≤ J · ∑ τₖ`  (exact case `ε = 0`: `QSL.Qudit.pi_div_four_le_entangling_time`)* `QSL.Qudit.pi_div_four_sub_le_entangling_time_shift_approx`: the same with `‖H − c • 1‖ ≤ J`* `QSL.Gate.two_arccos_sqrt_le_time_approx`: transferring `ψ₀` to a state of fidelity `≥ 1 − ε`  with an orthogonal unit vector `ψ₁` takes `2 · arccos √ε ≤ Ω · ∑ τₖ` Tightness (not formalized): with `H = (Ω/2) σₓ`, `ψ₀ = e₀`, `ψ₁ = e₁`, the transfer probability is`P₁(T) = sin² (Ω T / 2)`, so for `ε = cos² (Ω T / 2)` the bound `2 arccos √ε = Ω T` is attained. ## References * L.
