1 · The result

Theorem 3.1.

Let \(\lambda \) be dominant, \(\ell =\lambda +\rho \), \(\theta =p/q\) in lowest terms, and put

\[ N_q(x)=\# \{ \alpha {\gt}0:\ q\mid (x,\alpha )\} ,\qquad r_q=N_q(\rho ) . \]

Then \(N_q(\ell )\ge r_q\) for every \(\lambda \), and

\begin{equation} \boxed {\; \chi _\lambda (g_{p/q})\neq 0 \iff N_q(\ell )=r_q\; } \end{equation}

2 · In plain words

Take a dominant weight $\lambda$ and a rational point $g_{p/q}$ of the line through $\rho$. Count the positive roots $\alpha$ for which $q$ divides $(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)$, and the same count for $\rho$ alone. The first count is never smaller than the second, and the character is non-zero exactly when they are equal. So whether $\chi_\lambda(g_{p/q})$ vanishes is decided by divisibility of a few integers, without computing the character. At $a_P$ itself this is Corollary 3.2 of Cellini–M\"oseneder Frajria–Papi; what is new is the rest of the line, in particular the powers of $a_P$, where $\rho$ lies on walls and the alcove argument no longer applies.

3 · How it is proved

What the paper contributes

Kostant's element aP of Sp(2m) is one point of the line θ ↦ gθ = exp(2πiθρ♯), and at it every character is 0 or ±1. The paper studies the rest of the line, and above all the powers aPk, where ρ lies on root walls and the alcove argument no longer applies.

  • A classification (Theorem 5.1): every character of Sp(2m) is an integer at g1/q exactly for q | 2m, q | 2m+1, q | 2m+2, and q = 6 with m ≡ 1 (mod 3); the powers of aP are the family q | 2m+2.
  • A vanishing criterion at every rational point (Theorem 3.1): χλ(gp/q) ≠ 0 iff Nq(λ+ρ) = Nq(ρ); in type C, capacities of folded residue classes, with one unit of slack when d is even (Corollary 6.1, Proposition 6.2).
  • The value at every power, sign included (Theorem 7.1): (−1)Ep,q(λ) Zq(λ+ρ)/Zq(ρ), through a residue lemma that needs no pairing of roots (Proposition 9.1); its modulus is κ ∈ {1, 2} times dimensions of GLn, Sp2n and SO2n+1, one per folded class (Theorem 10.1); a closed count of the survivors for every q (Theorem 11.1); G2 at q = 3 (Corollary 12.1).

What the paper says is not new. At aP itself the criterion and the sign are Corollary 3.2 of Cellini–Möseneder Frajria–Papi; the coroot form is Corollary 3.7 of Nadimpalli–Pattanayak–Prasad; the principal specialisation is classical; at q = 2 the factorisation is Theorem 5.1 of Karmakar.

It rests on:

  • Weyl character formula and root systems — the principal specialisation read on the line of ρ (root statistic), and the order of vanishing of each factor at a root of unity
  • Galois theory of cyclotomic fields — integral characters force a Galois-stable spectrum; a strict inequality for Dirichlet kernels classifies the stable segments
  • Combinatorics of residues — folded residue classes modulo q, capacities by marginal cost, and the closed count of the survivors
  • Products of roots of unity — Vandermonde and deletion arguments; the residue identity ℛ_x = (P_x² + P_x(X²))/2 − m, the adjoint character minus the rank
  • Representations of classical groups — Weyl dimension formulas for GL_n, Sp_2n and SO_2n+1, one block per folded class
  • Formal verification — Lean 4 + Mathlib: the residue identity, the capacity count and the slack over ZMod q; the stable segments (Amiot)
