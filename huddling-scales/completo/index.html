Huddling, diatonic and pentatonic scales in Z12: Fourier maxima and convex-energy minima formalized in Lean 4

🏷️ Mathematical music theory · discrete Fourier analysis on ℤ₁₂

Carles Marín

At a glance

What the paper contributes

  • A connected Lean 4 formalization, with complete twelve-tone equality conditions, of why the seven white keys solve two optimization problems: they maximize $|\widehat A(5)|$ and minimize every strictly admissible convex pair energy (Theorem 3).
  • The route: Huddling in $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ (Theorem 1), transported to frequency five by $M_5$; double Abel summation for the energy (Theorem 2); complementation carries both to the pentatonic sets (Theorem 4).
  • Exact arithmetic throughout: comparisons in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt3]$, no rounded trigonometric values. Two finite censuses use native_decide (trusted compiled evaluation), stated in the axiom audit.
  • The mathematics is classical; the paper claims the connected formalization, not a new characterization.

📐 Setting (copied from the paper, §2)

For an unweighted set \(A\subseteq \mathbb {Z}_{12}\), use the unnormalized DFT

\[ \widehat A(t)=\sum _{a\in A}e^{-2\pi i at/12},\qquad S_t(A)=|\widehat A(t)|^2. \]

Transposition \(T_sA=A+s\) changes phase and preserves \(S_t\). For a unit \(u\in \mathbb {Z}_{12}^\times \), put \(M_uA=\{ ua:a\in A\} \). Reindexing the finite sum gives \(\widehat{M_uA}(t)=\widehat A(ut)\). For \(C_7=\{ 0,1,2,3,4,5,6\} \) and \(D=\{ 0,2,4,5,7,9,11\} \), \(T_{11}M_5C_7=D\) and \(M_5D=T_7C_7\). (§4) Write \(d(a,b)=\min ((a-b)\bmod 12,(b-a)\bmod 12)\). For a real potential \(V:\{ 1,\ldots ,6\} \to \mathbb R\), the all-pairs energy is \(E_V(A)=\sum _{\{ a,b\} \subseteq A}V(d(a,b))=\sum _{j=1}^{6}i_j(A)V(j)\). We call \(V\) admissible when \(\Delta ^2V(j):=V(j)-2V(j+1)+V(j+2)\ge 0\) \((1\le j\le 4)\) and \(V(6)\le V(5)\). Strict admissibility means that all five displayed inequalities are strict.

Abstract.

The diatonic collection solves two optimization problems on the twelve pitch classes: it maximizes the magnitude of the fifth discrete Fourier coefficient and minimizes an all-pairs energy for every decreasing, discretely convex potential. We formalize the connection in Lean 4. The route begins with the twelve-tone Huddling theorem: at each cardinality, consecutive pitch classes maximize the first Fourier magnitude, with equality exactly on their transposition orbit. Multiplication by five transports this maximum to frequency five; at cardinality seven, the resulting orbit is precisely the diatonic orbit. An Abel summation identity supplies the energy side. Under strict convexity and decrease, the two equality classes coincide: for every seven-note set \(A\), \(|{\widehat A}(5)|=2+\sqrt3\) if and only if \(E_V(A)=E_V(D)\). The contribution is a connected formalization of classical mathematics in \({\mathbb {Z}_{12}}\), with exact algebraic comparisons, explicit equality conditions, and independently reproducible cyclotomic checks. Two finite certificates use trusted compiled evaluation (native_decide); the axiom audit makes these dependencies explicit. A symbolic complement-energy identity transfers the result to five-note pentatonic sets without another census. Homometry, tritone transposition, and the parity coefficient connect the argument to the earlier notes.

Keywords: Huddling lemma; diatonic scale; pentatonic scale; pitch-class sets; discrete Fourier transform; maximal evenness; convex energy; Lean 4.

1 Why do the same seven notes appear twice?

The seven white keys of the piano give the pitch-class collection \(D=\{ 0,2,4,5,7,9,11\} \), with \(C=0\). On the twelve-position pitch-class clock they are spread around the circle. Read in a different order, they form a consecutive chain of fifths. These two views lead to two precise extremal properties of the same collection.

P0. Why does this seven-note collection maximize a Fourier magnitude and also minimize a repulsive pair energy?

The historical routes to the question were different. Lewin’s 1959 note on intervallic relations  [ 1 ] supplied an early Fourier viewpoint. Clough and Douthett’s 1991 study  [ 2 ] developed maximal evenness as a property of scales. Amiot recalls a more specific meeting of these ideas: the John Clough Memorial Days in Chicago in July 2005, where Quinn’s dissertation helped revive Lewin’s Fourier approach to chord quality  [ 5 ] . The occasion is a useful reminder that a change of mathematical language can make connections in familiar musical material visible.

Douthett and Krantz connected maximally even configurations to interaction energies and one-dimensional spin models  [ 6 ] . Amiot developed the Fourier characterization  [ 3 ] . This note follows the connection between those viewpoints at one fixed scale. Its thread is the diatonic collection throughout: first seen as a rearranged chromatic cluster, then as a Fourier maximizer, and finally as an energy minimizer. The Lean statements make the change of coordinates and the equality class explicit.

2 The change of coordinates

For an unweighted set \(A\subseteq {\mathbb {Z}_{12}}\), use the unnormalized DFT

\begin{equation} \tag {1} {\widehat A}(t)=\sum _{a\in A}e^{-2\pi i at/12},\qquad S_t(A)=|{\widehat A}(t)|^2. \end{equation}
1

In Lean, \(A\) is a Finset (ZMod 12) and the transform is Mathlib’s ZMod.dft applied to its indicator. The complex-valued Fourier.powerSpec has real part \(S_t(A)\); the bridge to the squared norm is Huddling.powerSpec_re_eq_norm_sq. Transposition \(T_sA=A+s\) changes phase and preserves \(S_t\).

P1. Which coordinates turn a consecutive chromatic cluster into a diatonic collection?

For a unit \(u\in {\mathbb {Z}_{12}}^\times \), put \(M_uA=\{ ua:a\in A\} \). Reindexing the finite sum gives

\begin{equation} \tag {2} \widehat{M_uA}(t)={\widehat A}(ut). \end{equation}
2

This is MTransform.Ahat_mul. Multiplication by five is its own inverse modulo twelve, so it preserves cardinality and exchanges frequencies one and five. Five semitones is a fourth, the reverse direction around the circle of fifths; this orientation has no effect on the transposition class at issue here. For \(C_7=\{ 0,1,2,3,4,5,6\} \),

\[ T_{11}M_5C_7=D,\qquad M_5D=T_7C_7. \]

Both identities are checked on the same set definitions used in the theorems.

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

Put the twelve pitch classes on a clock and give each note of \(A\) a unit arrow pointing at its hour; \(\widehat A(1)\) is the sum of the arrows and \(S_1(A)\) its squared length. Arrows pointing in nearly the same direction add up best, so among all sets with \(k\) notes the longest sum comes from \(k\) consecutive hours, a “huddled” arc, and only from the twelve rotations of that arc (one set when \(k=0\) or \(12\)). This is the twelve-tone instance of the roots-of-unity lemma recorded by Amiot; the paper does not claim it as new. What it adds is a proof without rounded cosines: it writes \(2S_1(A)=P(A)+Q(A)\sqrt3\) with integers \(P,Q\) counted from the intervals of \(A\), so every comparison is exact in \(\mathbb {Z}[\sqrt3]\), and it checks all \(2^{12}=4096\) sets.

P2. What does the cluster maximum say about the seven white keys?

Apply Theorem 1 to \(M_5A\) and use Equation (2). For \(|A|=7\) the result is

\begin{equation} \tag {4} S_5(A)\le S_5(D)=7+4\sqrt3, \qquad |{\widehat A}(5)|\le 2+\sqrt3. \end{equation}
4

Equality holds exactly on \({\mathcal O_D}=\{ T_sD:s\in {\mathbb {Z}_{12}}\} \). The inversions of \(D\) already belong to this orbit, so the transposition and \(T/I\) descriptions name the same twelve sets. This is why the word diatonic is appropriate for this Fourier extremum: the characterization singles out the collection up to transposition, rather than selecting a tonic or one of its modal orderings.

4 Why the energy selects the same collection

P3. Can the equality class be recovered by measuring separation between every pair of notes?

Write \(d(a,b)=\min ((a-b)\bmod 12,(b-a)\bmod 12)\). For a real potential \(V:\{ 1,\ldots ,6\} \to \mathbb R\), the all-pairs energy is

\begin{equation} \tag {5} E_V(A)=\sum _{\{ a,b\} \subseteq A}V(d(a,b)) =\sum _{j=1}^{6}i_j(A)V(j). \end{equation}
5

We call \(V\) admissible when

\begin{equation} \tag {6} \Delta ^2V(j):=V(j)-2V(j+1)+V(j+2)\ge 0\quad (1\le j\le 4), \qquad V(6)\le V(5). \end{equation}
6

These hypotheses imply that \(V\) is nonincreasing: its first differences are nondecreasing, and the last is nonpositive. Strict admissibility means that all five displayed inequalities are strict. No particular formula or positivity assumption on \(V\) is required.

The proof reuses the symbolic engine in AllPairsEvenness.lean. For \(|A|=7\), set \(d_j=i_{j+1}(A)-i_{j+1}(D)\), \(0\le j\le 5\), and

\[ H_j=\sum _{\ell =0}^{j}(j+1-\ell )d_\ell . \]

Both sets have \(\binom 72=21\) pairs, so \(\sum _{j=0}^5d_j=0\). Double Abel summation then gives

\begin{equation} \tag {7} E_V(A)-E_V(D)=\sum _{j=0}^{3}H_j\Delta ^2V(j+1) +H_4\bigl(V(5)-V(6)\bigr). \end{equation}
7

This identity is proved algebraically for arbitrary real \(V\), independently of the census. The finite input is that \(H_0,\ldots ,H_4\ge 0\) for every seven-note set, and some \(H_j{\gt}0\) outside \({\mathcal O_D}\). On \({\mathcal O_D}\) the whole interval vector agrees with that of \(D\). Equation (7) therefore establishes a minimum for every admissible potential, and identifies its equality class under the strict hypotheses.

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

Let every pair of notes of \(A\) repel each other with a strength \(V(d)\) that depends only on their distance \(d\) around the clock, and add over the \(21\) pairs of a seven-note set: that is the energy \(E_V(A)\). If closer pairs repel more (\(V\) nonincreasing) and the repulsion falls off in a convex way, the diatonic collection \(D\) has the least energy of all seven-note sets; if the falling-off is strictly convex, only its twelve transpositions do. The proof subtracts the two energies and, by summing by parts twice, writes the difference as a sum of the second differences of \(V\) times numbers \(H_j\) that depend only on the intervals of \(A\) and \(D\); a census shows \(H_j\ge 0\) for all \(792\) seven-note sets. Strictness matters: with \(V\) constant every seven-note set has energy \(21\) (the paper’s own example).

Strictness is essential to the equality statement. For the constant potential \(V=1\), every seven-note set has energy \(21\), while only twelve maximize \(S_5\). The weak minimum theorem thus cannot be substituted for the strict uniqueness theorem in the connection below.

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

Two questions about seven-note sets, with one answer. Which sets are most aligned with the circle of fifths, that is, have the largest fifth Fourier magnitude \(|\widehat A(5)|\)? Multiplying every note by \(5\) turns the circle of fifths into the chromatic circle, so Theorem 1 answers: the sets that become a consecutive arc, which are the twelve transpositions of the diatonic collection, with \(|\widehat A(5)|=2+\sqrt3\). Which sets have the least strictly convex pair energy? Theorem 2 answers: the same twelve. So for seven-note sets the two properties are the same property. The paper presents this as the conjunction of known characterizations (a corollary), formalized as one Lean theorem.

6 What happens to the five notes left over?

P4. Do the black keys inherit both extremal properties of the white keys?

Let \(P=\{ 0,2,4,7,9\} \) be the anhemitonic pentatonic collection. The five black keys form \(D^c=T_6P\). Complementation is already part of the Fourier language of Note 2  [ 10 ] : for \(t\ne 0\), \(\widehat{A^c}(t)=-{\widehat A}(t)\), so the magnitudes agree. This identity and the complement closure of maximal evenness are classical  [ 4 ] . The energy side requires keeping track of the unordered tritone convention. Our symbolic proof first counts common tones under transposition:

\[ |A^c\cap T_tA^c|+2|A|=12+|A\cap T_tA|. \]

Consequently, \(i_j(A^c)-i_j(A)=12-2|A|\) for \(1\le j\le 5\), and \(i_6(A^c)-i_6(A)=6-|A|\). Multiplying by the potential gives

\begin{equation} \tag {8} E_V(A^c)-E_V(A)=(6-|A|)\left(2\sum _{j=1}^5V(j)+V(6)\right). \end{equation}
8

No convexity is needed for this identity. It is the \(C_{12}\) instance of the stronger distance-degree-regular graph theorem of Bushaw, Cody, and Leffler  [ 7 , Theorem 3.5 ] . The new formal bridge exposes why the correction depends only on cardinality. For a finite abelian group \(G\) and an even potential \(f(x)=f(-x)\), set \(E_f(A)=\frac12\sum _{a,b\in A}f(b-a)\). Translation makes every row sum equal to \(R_f=\sum _{x\in G}f(x)\), and hence

\begin{equation} \tag {9} E_f(A^c)-E_f(A)=\left(\frac{|G|}{2}-|A|\right)R_f. \end{equation}
9

Equal-size sets therefore retain their energy gap under complementation; in particular, complementation transports cardinality-constrained minimizers. For \(G={\mathbb {Z}_{12}}\), \(R_f=V(0)+2\sum _{j=1}^5V(j)+V(6)\). Our interval energy \(E_V\) omits the diagonal; setting \(V(0)=0\) identifies its complement difference with Equation (9). A six-note set has the same energy as its complement for every circular-distance potential. These are classical consequences of symmetry, now linked to the exact twelve-tone convention in Lean. For \(|A|=5\), comparison with \(D\) and \(D^c=T_6P\) yields the exact gap identity

\[ E_V(A)-E_V(P)=E_V(A^c)-E_V(D). \]

Thus the five-note problem is obtained from the seven-note problem by a bijection, with the energy difference preserved and the fifth Fourier coefficient negated.

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

The five black keys are the complement of the seven white keys. Complementing a set only flips the sign of its Fourier coefficients away from frequency \(0\), so magnitudes are kept; and it changes the energy by an amount that depends only on how many notes the set has, \(E_V(A^c)-E_V(A)=(6-|A|)\bigl(2\sum _{j=1}^5V(j)+V(6)\bigr)\). So the five-note problem is the seven-note problem seen in a mirror: the pentatonic sets \(T_sP\) inherit both extremal properties, with no new census. The complement identity is a case of a theorem of Bushaw, Cody and Leffler for distance-degree-regular graphs, as the paper says.

The Lean proof uses the same two finite certificates as the diatonic result. The complement bridge itself is symbolic. This also closes the opening piano image: the white and black keys solve complementary problems, rather than providing unrelated examples of regularity.

7 What the earlier notes can now say

P5. Can a measurement that forgets phase still identify this scale?

Note 2 studies homometry: equality of autocorrelation, equivalently of Fourier power. Our bridge between its ordered interval counts and the unordered counts in Equation (5) proves

\[ A\text{ and }B\text{ homometric}\quad \Longrightarrow \quad E_V(A)=E_V(B) \quad \text{for every }V. \]

This is a consequence of the classical equivalent descriptions of homometry  [ 8 ] . Moreover, homometry survives complementation: the autocorrelation identity above changes every nonzero displacement count by the same cardinality-dependent constant. Thus homometric \(A,B\) also have homometric complements, whose pair energies agree for every potential. This joins the phase question to the complement bridge without an additional finite census. For general sets, pair energies cannot recover phase or distinguish a nontrivial homometric pair. At the diatonic extremum, however, spectral uniqueness implies

\[ A\text{ homometric to }D\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad A\in {\mathcal O_D}. \]

The equality class is rigid enough that forgetting phase leaves only the expected transposition ambiguity. This corollary links the earlier phase question directly to extremality; it is not claimed as a new mathematical theorem.

The same coordinate map also links the tritone of Note 1  [ 11 ] to the parity channel of Note 4  [ 9 ] . Since \(5\cdot 6=6\pmod{12}\),

\[ \widehat{M_5A}(6)={\widehat A}(6),\qquad M_5(T_6A)=T_6(M_5A). \]

Thus \(M_5\) exchanges the chromatic and diatonic Fourier coordinates while preserving the sixth coefficient and commuting with the central tritone transposition. These identities do not assert that a diatonic set has the 6-30 stabilizer, or explain a duration-weighted corpus effect by themselves.

Finally, Note 3’s Tonnetz spectrum  [ 12 ] contains \(2\cos (\pi /12)\) as the fifth-frequency magnitude of a generating triad. Here the extremal seven-note scale has magnitude \(2+\sqrt3\). The common Fourier coordinate connects the measurements; their different cardinalities explain the different constants.

9 Novelty, scope, and what this note does not claim

Huddling, the Fourier index permutation, maximal evenness, and the energy viewpoint are classical  [ 3 , 5 , 2 , 6 ] . The contribution here is their connected formalization with the complete twelve-tone equality conditions, and the explicit theorem joining the spectral and energy optimizers. The conjunction of published equality-class characterizations is a corollary, not a new characterization. Complement-energy transport is likewise covered in greater generality by Bushaw, Cody, and Leffler  [ 7 , Theorems 2.16 and 3.5 ] ; the homometry and parity connections follow from classical identities. It extends the earlier Fourier and Abel machinery in this project.

The statement-level literature audit is recorded in literature_novelty_2026-10-03.json. It separates published theorems, their consequences, and the question of proof-assistant implementation. The targeted review establishes positive antecedents for the central mathematics; it does not claim an exhaustive survey of every manuscript.

A dated overlap audit searched the October 3, 2026 Mathlib source snapshot (\(8592\) Lean files) and the checked Prismriver snapshot (\(35\) Lean files), inspected their relevant Fourier and music modules, and ran indexed Huddling searches for Lean, Coq/Rocq, Isabelle, and Agda. No matching formalization was located in those sources. The audit records revisions, queries, and coverage in formalization_prior_art_2026-10-03.json; a negative indexed search cannot establish absence from every public or private codebase. Related Christoffel-word results do exist in another Lean project and require a separate overlap review.

All Huddling statements in this note use \({\mathbb {Z}_{12}}\); the joined energy statements fix cardinalities seven and five. We do not infer an arbitrary-universe formal theorem from this finite census, or identify the energy optimizer with the fifth-frequency optimizer at every other cardinality. The theorem concerns unweighted sets, not melodic order, tonics, acoustic consonance, or the historical origin of the diatonic scale.

Coda: where the question leads

The starting question has an exact answer: the seven white keys belong to one orbit selected by two different objective functions, and \(M_5\) connects the Fourier maximum to a consecutive cluster. The resulting formalization leaves a concrete next question.

Can the finite Huddling certificate be replaced by a structural Lean proof for arbitrary roots of unity, retaining the full equality condition?

The mathematical theorem already exists; the next task is to make its geometric argument reusable in a proof assistant. Once that is available, the same coordinate change can be studied for other cyclic universes. A further question is then precise rather than metaphorical: which Fourier frequency and cardinality select the same orbit as a given convex-energy problem? The present seven-of-twelve result supplies one checked instance and a shared interface on which to build.
