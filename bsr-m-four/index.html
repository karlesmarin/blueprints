Every m×4 biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of 3m−1 squares (5 ≤ m ≤ 16)

🏷️ Real algebraic geometry · sums of squares on projective varieties

Carles Marín, Liqun Qi, Chen Ouyang

At a glance

What the paper contributes

BSR(m,4) ≤ 3m − 1 for 5 ≤ m ≤ 16 (Theorem 1): every real m×4 biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of at most 3m − 1 squares of bilinear forms, one square below the previous bound 3m. In particular BSR(5,4) ≤ 14, the upper half of the conjecture BSR(5,4) = 14 of Qi and Ouyang; with the known lower bounds, 13 ≤ BSR(5,4) ≤ 14 and 16 ≤ BSR(6,4) ≤ 17. The paper does not decide whether BSR(5,4) is 13 or 14.

  • A boundary inertia inequality (Lemma 17): at a boundary point of the sums of N squares inside the cone, the negative index q of the Hankel multiplier satisfies N(q − 1) ≤ dim R2 − C(N+2, 2). The paper says it is, in substance, Theorem 3.4 of Boumal, Voroninski and Bandeira, transported to this setting; its case q = 1 recovers the Barvinok–Pataki bound. For m×4 forms it gives q ≤ ⌊(m+13)/6⌋, which is at most 4 exactly for m ≤ 16.
  • A case analysis over the support triples (a, b, r) (Theorem 25): compression to fewer rows for a ≤ m − 2, with the base values BSR(k,4) ≤ 3k − 1 for k = 2, 3, 4; a four-row isotropic lemma (Lemma 20) and a low-negative-index lemma for q ≤ 4 (Lemma 23) for the rest.
  • Explicit regular tuples for every m (Lemma 24) and a boundary selection lemma whose codimension-two hypothesis is sharp (Lemma 12).
  • Where the method stops: at m = 17 the inertia bound allows q = 5.
  • Machine checks: the arithmetic, the enumeration of cases and several linear-algebra steps are checked in Isabelle/HOL (six sessions) or by exact computation; these checks cover individual steps, not the whole theorem.

📐 Setting (copied from the paper: abstract, §2 and §3)

Let \(\operatorname {BSR}(m,n)\) be the least \(r\) such that every real biquadratic sum of squares in \(m+n\) variables is a sum of \(r\) squares of bilinear forms. (Abstract.)

Put \(A=\mathbb R^5\), \(B=\mathbb R^4\), \(V=A\otimes B\), and

\[ R_2=\operatorname {Sym}^2A\otimes \operatorname {Sym}^2B,\quad \Phi _k:V^k\longrightarrow R_2,\quad \Phi _k(h)=\sum _{i=1}^kh_i^2. \]

Thus \(\dim V=20\) and \(\dim R_2=150\). Write \(\Sigma _k=\operatorname {im}\Phi _k\), \(\Sigma =\bigcup _k\Sigma _k\). For \(F\in \Sigma \), the least \(k\) with \(F\in \Sigma _k\) is \(\operatorname {SOS}_{\mathbb R}(F)\); \(\operatorname {BSR}(m,n)\) is the largest real SOS length in size \(m\times n\). (§2)

A Hankel multiplier is a symmetric bilinear form \(H\) on \(V\) with

\[ H(x\otimes b,y\otimes c)=H(x\otimes c,y\otimes b). \tag {H} \]

Such forms are precisely the functionals on \(R_2\), evaluated on products of bilinear forms. The first radical consists of the \(x\) such that \(H(x\otimes B,V)=0\); the first support is its codimension. Define the second radical and second support in the other factor. The image of \(H:V\to V^*\) is called product-free if its complexification contains no nonzero decomposable covector. (§2)

Factor a rank-\(r\) multiplier as

\[ H=T^*JT,\qquad T:A\otimes B\to E\text{ onto},\quad \dim E=r, \]

where \(J\) is nondegenerate symmetric. Set \(T_bx=T(x\otimes b)\); write \(\langle \, ,\, \rangle \) for \(J\). Condition (H) becomes

\[ \langle T_bx,T_cy\rangle =\langle T_cx,T_by\rangle . \tag {F} \]

(§2)

… extend to \(m\times 4\) biquadratic forms after the following modifications, with \(14\) replaced by \(N=3m-1\), \(\dim V=20\) by \(4m\), \(\dim R_2=150\) by \(M=5m(m+1)\), the bound \(r\le 6\) by \(r\le 4m-N=m+1\), and \(\operatorname {BSR}(5-a,4)\) by \(\operatorname {BSR}(m-a,4)\) … Throughout, effective supports \((a,b)\) means the first and second supports of \(H\) (codimensions of the two radicals). (§3)

Throughout the argument, \(q\) denotes the negative index of this oriented multiplier. The orientation is fixed by

\[ \sum _iH(D_i,D_i)\ge 0 \quad \hbox{for every syzygy }\sum _i h_iD_i=0. \]

(§3.1, «Orientation and inertia».)

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

Take a biquadratic form in \(x_1,\dots ,x_m\) and \(y_1,\dots ,y_4\) that is a sum of squares of bilinear forms, however many. The theorem says that \(3m-1\) squares always suffice when \(5\le m\le 16\): \(14\) for \(m=5\), \(47\) for \(m=16\). The previous bound was \(3m\), obtained from \(\operatorname {BSR}(m,3)=2m\) by adding one column. For \(m=5\) this is the upper half of the conjecture \(\operatorname {BSR}(5,4)=14\) of Qi and Ouyang; with the lower bound \(13\) it leaves \(13\le \operatorname {BSR}(5,4)\le 14\), and the paper does not decide which.

The proof does not search for representations. If the sums of \(N=3m-1\) squares were not the whole cone, a boundary point inside the cone would carry a Hankel multiplier \(H\) that kills every summand (so its rank is at most \(4m-N=m+1\)), whose image contains no nonzero decomposable covector \(\alpha \otimes \beta \), even over \(\mathbb C\) (product-free), and whose negative index \(q\) is at most \(\lfloor (m+13)/6\rfloor \) by the boundary inertia inequality (Lemma 17). The proof lists the possible triples of row support, column support and rank of \(H\) and excludes each one (Theorem 25). It stops at \(m=16\) because at \(m=17\) the inertia bound allows \(q=5\), and the low-negative-index lemma is proved only for \(q\le 4\).

The arithmetic, the enumeration of the cases and several linear-algebra steps are checked in Isabelle/HOL or by exact computation; in the paper’s words, these checks cover individual steps, not the whole theorem.

Proof ▼

For \(5\le m\le 16\), adding one column (see below) gives \({\operatorname {BSR}}(m,5)\le m+{\operatorname {BSR}}(m,4)\le m+3m-1\). For \(m\ge 12\), the bound \(\Lambda \) of [ 22 ] (the largest \(r\) with \(\binom {r+1}2\le \dim R_2=15\binom {m+1}2\)) is at most \(4m-1\), since \(r=4m\) would need \(4m(4m+1)\le 15m(m+1)\), that is, \(m\le 11\). For \(m=2,3,4\), symmetry gives \({\operatorname {BSR}}(m,5)={\operatorname {BSR}}(5,m)\), which is \(6\), \(10\) and at most \(14\) by [ 24 ] , [ 3 ] and Theorem 1.

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

Take a point \(F\) of the sums of \(N\) squares that lies inside the cone and where they have a one-sided \(C^2\) defining function \(g\) (\(g(F)=0\), \(dg_F\ne 0\), \(g\ge 0\) on \(\Sigma _N\) near \(F\)): a boundary point. The differential gives a symmetric form \(H(u,w)=dg_F(uw)\) on the forms. The lemma bounds its negative index \(q\) (with the orientation fixed by the second-order condition): \(Nq\le M-1-\binom {N+1}2\), equivalently \(N(q-1)\le M-\binom {N+2}2\), with \(M=\dim R_2\).

Why it is true: every representation \(F=\sum h_i^2\) is a local minimum of \(g\circ \Phi _N\), so \(H\) kills the summands and is nonnegative, summed over the \(N\) slots, along every syzygy \(\sum _ih_iD_i=0\). There are at least \(Nv-M+1\) independent syzygies; after projecting through \(T\) at least \(Nr+\binom {N+1}2-M+1\) of them survive, and a space on which \(J^{\oplus N}\) is nonnegative has dimension at most \(Np\). Writing \(r=p+q\) gives the inequality. Each negative direction beyond the first costs \(N\) dimensions of syzygies.

For \(q=1\) it says that the sums of \(N\) squares have a boundary inside the cone only if \(\binom {N+2}2\le M\): the Barvinok–Pataki bound. For \(m\times 4\) forms and \(N=3m-1\) the room \(M-\binom {N+2}2\) is exactly \(m(m+7)/2\), and the bound becomes \(q\le \lfloor (m+13)/6\rfloor \) for \(m\ge 5\): \(3\) for \(m=5,\dots ,10\), \(4\) for \(m=11,\dots ,16\), \(5\) at \(m=17\) (the Isabelle lemma q_values).

💡 In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper)

This is Theorem 1 proved for every \(5\le m\le 16\) at once, by induction on \(m\). The paper’s paragraph «The mechanism in three counts» says why it works and where it stops.

(a) The rank budget. The summands of a shortest representation span an \(N\)-dimensional space killed by \(H\), so \(\operatorname {rank}H\le \dim V-N=4m-(3m-1)=m+1\).

(b) The room for negative directions. By the boundary inertia inequality (Lemma 17), \(q\le q_*=1+\frac{m(m+7)}{2(3m-1)}\); the lemmas of Section 3 handle \(q\le 4\), and \(\lfloor q_*\rfloor \le 4\) exactly for \(m\le 16\) (\(q_*=22/7\) at \(m=5\), \(231/47\) at \(m=16\), \(127/25\) at \(m=17\)).

(c) The induction line. Compression puts all but at most \(4a-r\) summands on the \(m-a\) rows outside the row support, so \(\operatorname {SOS}_{\mathbb R}(F)\le 4a-r+\operatorname {BSR}(m-a,4)\); when \(a\le m-2\), with \(r\ge a+1\) and \(\operatorname {BSR}(k,4)\le 3k-1\) for \(k=m-a\ge 2\), this is at most \(3m-2{\lt}N\), a contradiction. The known values \(5,8,11\) for \(k=2,3,4\) lie exactly on the line \(3k-1\), so the induction closes; it cannot start from \(k=1\), since \(\operatorname {BSR}(1,4)=4{\gt}2\). That is why the row supports \(a=m-1\) and \(a=m\) are left to the isotropic and low-index lemmas.

What remains: the low-index lemma (\(r\ge a+2\) when \(b=4\)) removes \((m,4,m+1)\) and \((m-1,4,m)\), and the four triples \((m-1,4,m+1)\), \((m,3,m+1)\), \((m-1,3,m+1)\), \((m-1,3,m)\) are excluded one by one, by the rank of the alternating map \(\Psi \) against the positive index, or by a syzygy whose second variation is negative. Suppose \(\dim A=d\), \(\dim E=d+1\), a generic \(T_b\) is injective, and every \(Q(b)\) is singular. Choose a Witt decomposition

\[ E=W\perp \langle e,f\rangle ,\quad \dim W=d-1,\quad e^2=f^2=0,\quad \langle e,f\rangle =1, \]

Proof ▼

Suppress the subscript \(c\). The blocks of \(Q(b+\tau c)\) are the following exact multiplication, computation \(\mathrm{C2}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal M& =I+2\tau A+\tau ^2(A^2+\ell p^t+p\ell ^t),\\ \nu & =2\tau p+\tau ^2(Ap+tp+\eta \ell ),\\ \omega & =2\tau \eta +\tau ^2(p^tp+2t\eta ). \end{aligned} \]

Since \(\mathcal M(0)=I\), the formal Schur complement \(\omega -\nu ^t\mathcal M^{-1}\nu \) is defined. With \(\nu =\tau \nu _1+\tau ^2\nu _2\), \(\nu _1=2p\), \(\nu _2=Ap+tp+\eta \ell \) and \(\mathcal M^{-1}=I-2\tau A+O(\tau ^2)\),

\[ \nu ^t\mathcal M^{-1}\nu =4\tau ^2p^tp+\tau ^3\bigl(2\nu _1^t\nu _2-2\nu _1^tA\nu _1\bigr)+O(\tau ^4) =4\tau ^2p^tp+\tau ^3\bigl(-4p^tAp+4tp^tp+4\eta p^t\ell \bigr)+O(\tau ^4), \]

and \(\omega \) has no \(\tau ^3\) term. Hence the first three coefficients of \(\omega -\nu ^t\mathcal M^{-1}\nu \) are

\[ 2\eta ,\quad 2t\eta -3p^tp,\quad 4p^tAp-4tp^tp-4\eta p^t\ell . \]

Its vanishing follows from \(\det Q(b+\tau c)=0\). Successively setting these coefficients to zero proves (6).
