Theorem 1.

💬 In plain words 2 / 5

Take a biquadratic form in $x_1,\dots,x_m$ and $y_1,\dots,y_4$ that is a sum of squares of bilinear forms, however many. The theorem says that $3m-1$ squares always suffice when $5\le m\le16$: $14$ for $m=5$, $47$ for $m=16$. The previous bound was $3m$, obtained from $\operatorname{BSR}(m,3)=2m$ by adding one column. For $m=5$ this is the upper half of the conjecture $\operatorname{BSR}(5,4)=14$ of Qi and Ouyang; with the lower bound $13$ it leaves $13\le\operatorname{BSR}(5,4)\le14$, and the paper does not decide which.

The proof does not search for representations. If the sums of $N=3m-1$ squares were not the whole cone, a boundary point inside the cone would carry a Hankel multiplier $H$ that kills every summand (so its rank is at most $4m-N=m+1$), whose image contains no nonzero decomposable covector $\alpha\otimes\beta$, even over $\mathbb C$ (product-free), and whose negative index $q$ is at most $\lfloor(m+13)/6\rfloor$ by the boundary inertia inequality (Lemma 17). The proof lists the possible triples of row support, column support and rank of $H$ and excludes each one (Theorem 25). It stops at $m=16$ because at $m=17$ the inertia bound allows $q=5$, and the low-negative-index lemma is proved only for $q\le4$.

The arithmetic, the enumeration of the cases and several linear-algebra steps are checked in Isabelle/HOL or by exact computation; in the paper's words, these checks cover individual steps, not the whole theorem.

🛠️ How it is proved 3 / 5

What the paper contributes

BSR(m,4) ≤ 3m − 1 for 5 ≤ m ≤ 16 (Theorem 1): every real m×4 biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of at most 3m − 1 squares of bilinear forms, one square below the previous bound 3m. In particular BSR(5,4) ≤ 14, the upper half of the conjecture BSR(5,4) = 14 of Qi and Ouyang; with the known lower bounds, 13 ≤ BSR(5,4) ≤ 14 and 16 ≤ BSR(6,4) ≤ 17. The paper does not decide whether BSR(5,4) is 13 or 14.

  • A boundary inertia inequality (Lemma 17): at a boundary point of the sums of N squares inside the cone, the negative index q of the Hankel multiplier satisfies N(q − 1) ≤ dim R2 − C(N+2, 2). The paper says it is, in substance, Theorem 3.4 of Boumal, Voroninski and Bandeira, transported to this setting; its case q = 1 recovers the Barvinok–Pataki bound. For m×4 forms it gives q ≤ ⌊(m+13)/6⌋, which is at most 4 exactly for m ≤ 16.
  • A case analysis over the support triples (a, b, r) (Theorem 25): compression to fewer rows for a ≤ m − 2, with the base values BSR(k,4) ≤ 3k − 1 for k = 2, 3, 4; a four-row isotropic lemma (Lemma 20) and a low-negative-index lemma for q ≤ 4 (Lemma 23) for the rest.
  • Explicit regular tuples for every m (Lemma 24) and a boundary selection lemma whose codimension-two hypothesis is sharp (Lemma 12).
  • Where the method stops: at m = 17 the inertia bound allows q = 5.
  • Machine checks: the arithmetic, the enumeration of cases and several linear-algebra steps are checked in Isabelle/HOL (six sessions) or by exact computation; these checks cover individual steps, not the whole theorem.

It rests on:

  • Boundary strategy for Pythagoras numbers (Blekherman–Dunbar–Sinn) — a one-sided boundary point of the sums of 3m − 1 squares inside the cone, off the singular incidence (codimension two), carries an indefinite Hankel multiplier with product-free image and nonnegative second variation on syzygies
  • Second-order conditions (Burer–Monteiro, Boumal–Voroninski–Bandeira) — counting syzygies bounds the negative index of the multiplier: N(q − 1) ≤ dim R₂ − C(N+2, 2), whose case q = 1 is the Barvinok–Pataki bound
  • Linear algebra over C — pencils, totally isotropic subspaces, simultaneous triangularisation with commutators into a line, linear spaces of matrices of rank at most one, formal Schur complements and their moments
  • Completely entangled subspaces (Parthasarathy) — a product-free image has rank at most (a − 1)(b − 1)
