🎯 The result 1 / 5

Theorem 1.

For a monic real-rooted real polynomial \(p\) of degree \(n\) and every \(1\le k\le n-1\),

\(\displaystyle e_k^2\, \binom {n}{k-1}\binom {n}{k+1}\; \ge \; e_{k-1}\, e_{k+1}\, \binom {n}{k}^{2}.\)

Equivalently (newton_logConcave) the normalized means \(p_k=e_k/\binom {n}{k}\) are log-concave, \(p_{k-1}\, p_{k+1}\le p_k^2\). Both are sorry-free; #print axioms on each reports only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

💬 In plain words 2 / 5

1. If all roots of a polynomial are real, its coefficients cannot be arbitrary: the normalized means $p_k=e_k/\binom{n}{k}$ satisfy $p_{k-1}p_{k+1}\le p_k^2$, so on a log scale they bow inward. For $(x-1)(x-2)(x-4)=x^3-7x^2+14x-8$ the two inequalities read $147\ge126$ and $588\ge504$; $(x-1)^4$ gives equality everywhere, and $x^2+1$, whose roots are not real, fails ($0\not\ge4$). 2. Why it is true (the classical proof, here checked by Lean): differentiation (Rolle) and reversal $x\mapsto1/x$ both keep all roots real. Differentiating $i-1$ times, reversing and differentiating again leaves a real-rooted polynomial of degree at most $2$ whose three coefficients are positive factorial multiples of $a_{i-1},a_i,a_{i+1}$; its discriminant is $\ge0$, and that is the inequality once the factorials are cleared. The base case is stated with $\deg\le2$, which also covers $a_{i-1}=0$ without a separate case. 3. New and not new, as the paper says: the inequalities are Newton's (1707), proved by Sylvester, in Hardy–Littlewood–P\'olya; the paper claims the formalization, to the author's knowledge the first in any proof assistant (§6). Lean checks the symmetric-function form for monic $p$ (no loss: the $e_k$ depend only on the roots) and the coefficient form with no normalization; the equality cases and Maclaurin's chain are not proved.

🛠️ How it is proved 3 / 5

What the paper contributes

A machine-checked, sorry-free proof in Lean 4 over Mathlib of Newton's inequalities (1707): for a real-rooted real polynomial of degree n, ek2·C(n,k−1)·C(n,k+1) ≥ ek−1·ek+1·C(n,k)2 for 1 ≤ k ≤ n−1; equivalently the normalized means pk = ek/C(n,k) are log-concave.

  • The theorem (Theorem 1): the symmetric-function form assumes p monic; the coefficient form newton_inequality_coeff assumes nothing.
  • The route is the classical one (Sylvester; Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya): differentiate, reverse, differentiate again, down to a real-rooted quadratic, whose discriminant is the inequality once positive factorials are cleared.
  • Four reusable blocks: real-rootedness under differentiation, iterated differentiation and reversal, and the coefficient-form discriminant for degree ≤ 2, which also absorbs the degenerate case ai−1 = 0.
  • Boundary: the mathematics is classical; the paper claims the formalization (to the author's knowledge the first in any proof assistant, June 2026). The equality cases and Maclaurin's chain are named, not proved.

It rests on:

  • Real-rootedness preservers — Rolle's theorem for polynomials (real roots survive differentiation) and reversal x ↦ 1/x
  • Symmetric functions — Vieta's relations between coefficients and the elementary symmetric functions of the roots (Mathlib's Multiset.esymm)
  • Formal verification — Lean 4 + Mathlib: falling factorials of iterated derivatives, exact degrees in characteristic zero
