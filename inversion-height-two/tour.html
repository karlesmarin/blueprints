1 · The result

.

Let \(\Sigma \) be a family of full matrices over \(P\) and \(H\) a full square matrix over \(P\) with an atomic factor \(G\) in a complete factorization. If for every \(Q\in \Sigma \) there is a monotone invariant \(\mu _Q\) (Definition 3.3) with \(\mu _Q(G){\gt}\mu _Q(Q)\), then \(H^{-1}\) does not have entries in \(U_\Sigma \).

2 · In plain words

\paragraph{In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper).} 1. To show that an element is not in a ring generated by inverting a family $§igma$ of matrices, one has to exclude every formula. Lemma 3.1 reduces this to atoms: if $H^{-1}$ has its entries in $U_§igma$, every atomic factor of $H$ is stably associated with an atomic factor of a single $Q\in§igma$. The criterion then compares numbers: a monotone invariant (Definition 3.3) does not change under stable association and does not grow when one passes to a factor, so if one atomic factor $G$ of $H$ scores higher than $Q$, for every $Q\in§igma$, then $H^{-1}$ is not in $U_§igma$. 2. The paper uses three invariants: the number $\nu_j$ of Smith invariant factors of order $\ge j$ along an arc on which $u^2,v^2,w^2$ become the same scalar (§4.1); the corank on a curve of quaternions on which every binomial is invertible (§4.2); and the corank at the character $\chi\colon x_j\mapsto-1$. The invariant may be chosen separately for each $Q$, and it need not vanish on the allowed denominators: it only has to be smaller there than on the target. That is what makes the criterion work inside $E_2$, where the allowed denominators are singular at the evaluations that detect the witnesses. 3. New and not new, as the paper says: the paper found no use of Smith profiles along an arc, nor of coranks of atoms at a representation where the allowed denominators are singular, to prove non-membership (§8.8). Lemma 3.1 is the analogue, for $U_§igma$ and without factor-completeness, of results of Cohn, and the correspondence behind it is not claimed (Remark 8.6); kernel dimensions already separate stable association classes [SV26].

3 · How it is proved

What the paper contributes

The free field D of A = ℂ[F3] is built in stages: E1 inverts the nonzero elements of A, E2 those of E1, and so on. The stages never stabilise (Reutenauer; Herbera–Sánchez). The paper works inside the single stage E2, where one restricts which elements are inverted.

  • A non-membership criterion (Theorem A = Theorem 3.4). If H−1 has its entries in the ring UΣ generated by the inverses of a family Σ of full matrices, each atomic factor of H is stably associated with an atomic factor of some Q ∈ Σ (Lemma 3.1). To refute that, it suffices that one atomic factor of H be more singular than every Q at some evaluation, measured by an invariant that does not grow on factors: Smith counts along an arc, the corank on a curve of integral quaternions, the corank at a character. The allowed denominators may themselves be singular there.
  • Two hierarchies inside E2, strict at every step (Theorem B). The rings 𝒰r generated by inverses of sums of products of at most r inverse binomials: 𝒰r−1 ⊊ 𝒰r, with witness (u−2+v−2+w−2)−1 for r = 2; their union is a proper subring of E2. The matrix-size chain Ad−1 ∩ E2 ⊊ Ad ∩ E2 for every d ≥ 2. The two hierarchies are independent, and the size chain is already strict inside 𝒰2 (Corollary 7.10).
  • Infinitely many stopping steps of the Dubrovin–Gräter–Hanke series inside E2 (Theorem C): for every k ≥ 3 the stratum of sums of k elements of Dπ, with explicit witnesses fk. That there are infinitely many stopping steps follows from Herbera–Sánchez, but no infinite family had been located within a fixed stage. The rings reached at these steps form a strict chain whose union is 𝒰2.

Main tools: Cohn's theory of firs, atoms and stable association, and of rational closures; Smith normal forms over ℂ[[t]] along an exact arc of 2×2 matrices; the free group generated by 1+2i, 1+2j, 1+2k; a grading argument that gives an atom for every k.

It rests on:

  • Theory of firs (Cohn) — complete factorization into atoms, stable association, torsion modules and rational closures
  • Linear algebra over power series — Smith normal forms over ℂ[[t]] along an exact arc of 2×2 matrices
  • Arithmetic of quaternions — the free group generated by 1+2i, 1+2j, 1+2k (Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak), residues modulo 5
  • Ordered groups — a grading argument and a bi-invariant order on the free group, to obtain an atom for every k
  • Formal verification — Lean 4 + Mathlib: the finite identities and certificates of the appendix
