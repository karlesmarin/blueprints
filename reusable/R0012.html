/-- `X² − 1` splits over `ℝ`: it has degree `2` and a root in each gap of `−2 < 0 < 2`. -/
example : (X ^ 2 - 1 : Polynomial ℝ).Splits := by
  refine splits_of_isRoot_mem_Ioo_of_gaps _ ?_ 2 ?_ ![-2, 0, 2] ?_ ?_
  · intro h
    have := congrArg (eval 0) h
    simp at this
  · compute_degree!
  · refine Fin.strictMono_iff_lt_succ.2 fun i => ?_
    fin_cases i <;> norm_num
  · intro i
    fin_cases i
    · exact ⟨-1, by norm_num, by norm_num, by norm_num [IsRoot.def]⟩
    · exact ⟨1, by norm_num, by norm_num, by norm_num [IsRoot.def]⟩

    Steps 3 and 4 together are a complete Lean file:FormalizationsTest/ReusablePolynomialExample.lean at v0.2.0, compiled by the CI.

Avoid a bare lake build in a project from the standard template: its default target is the executable, whose link compiles every imported Mathlib module to native code. Lean docstring

Real-rootedness from one root per gap. If a nonzero real polynomial f of degree at most n has a root in each of the n open gaps (x i, x (i+1)) between n + 1 strictly increasing points, then f splits over ℝ. This is the closing step of the interlacing proofs of real-rootedness (orthogonal polynomials through their three-term recurrence; Cauchy interlacing of characteristic polynomials).

  • Archive: MSS.realRooted_of_root_per_gap (Formalizations.Analysis.Polynomial.RealRoots.RealRooted); restated with Polynomial.Splits through realRooted_iff_splits. The module cites no paper DOI of ours.
  • Classical reference: S. Fisk, Polynomials, roots, and interlacing, arXiv:math/0612833.
  • Example: X² − 1 has degree 2 and roots in (−2, 0) and (0, 2), so splits_of_isRoot_mem_Ioo_of_gaps shows that it splits.
