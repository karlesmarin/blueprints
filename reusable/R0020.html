Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem: over an integral domain, deleting the row and column of any vertex from the Laplacian (Mathlib's lapMatrix) of a finite simple graph leaves a matrix whose determinant is the number of spanning trees.

Lean 4
theorem det_lapMatrix_submatrix_eq_card_spanningTrees {V : Type*} (R : Type*) [CommRing R]
    [IsDomain R] (G : SimpleGraph V) [Fintype V] [LinearOrder V] [DecidableRel G.Adj] (v₀ : V) :
    ((G.lapMatrix R).submatrix (Subtype.val : {v : V // v ≠ v₀} → V) Subtype.val).det
      = ((Finset.univ.filter
          (fun S : {s : Finset (Sym2 V) // s.card = Fintype.card {v : V // v ≠ v₀}} =>
            ↑S.1 ⊆ G.edgeSet ∧ (SimpleGraph.fromEdgeSet (↑S.1 : Set (Sym2 V))).IsTree)).card : R)

Lean docstring

Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem. Let G be a simple graph on a finite linearly ordered vertex type V, v₀ : V and R an integral domain. Deleting the row and the column of v₀ from Mathlib's Laplacian G.lapMatrix R leaves a matrix whose determinant is the number of spanning trees of G, counted as the sets of |V| − 1 edges of G whose spanning subgraph (SimpleGraph.fromEdgeSet) is a tree. The linear order on V only fixes the orientation used in the proof (oriented incidence matrix and Cauchy–Binet); any order gives the same count.

  • Archive: SimpleGraph.det_reducedLapMatrix_eq_card_spanningTrees (Formalizations.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.MatrixTree.Kirchhoff, with the oriented incidence matrix and the Cauchy–Binet formula of the same archive); the archive's reducedLapMatrix is (G.lapMatrix R).submatrix Subtype.val Subtype.val by definition. The module cites no paper DOI of ours. Mathlib's SimpleGraph/IncMatrix.lean lists the oriented incidence matrix and the Laplacian as a TODO.
  • Classical reference: G. Kirchhoff, Ann. Phys. 148 (1847) 497–508; N. Biggs, Algebraic Graph Theory, 2nd ed. (1993), ch. 6.
  • Example: for the complete graph on Fin 3 (a triangle) and v₀ = 0, the reduced Laplacian is !![2, -1; -1, 2] with determinant 3, the three spanning trees of the triangle.
