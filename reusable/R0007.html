/-- `X² − 1` splits over `ℝ`: its signs at `−2, 0, 2` are `+, −, +`. -/
example : (X ^ 2 - 1 : Polynomial ℝ).Splits := by
  refine splits_of_alternating_sign _ ?_ 2 ?_ ![-2, 0, 2] ?_ ?_
  · intro h
    have := congrArg (eval 0) h
    simp at this
  · compute_degree!
  · refine Fin.strictMono_iff_lt_succ.2 fun i => ?_

  · intro i
    fin_cases i <;> norm_num

Lean docstring

Real-rootedness from sign alternation. A nonzero real polynomial h of degree at most n that alternates sign at n + 1 strictly increasing points x 0 < x 1 < ⋯ < x n (h (x i) · h (x (i+1)) < 0) splits over ℝ: the intermediate value theorem gives a root in each of the n gaps, and the degree bound leaves no room for non-real roots. This is the step in which the method of interlacing polynomials (Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava) concludes real-rootedness.

  • Archive: MSS.realRooted_of_alternating (Formalizations.Analysis.Polynomial.RealRoots.RealRooted, a tool for the Heilmann–Lieb theorem on matching polynomials in the same archive); restated with Polynomial.Splits through realRooted_iff_splits.
  • Classical reference: S. Fisk, Polynomials, roots, and interlacing, arXiv:math/0612833; A. W. Marcus, D. A. Spielman, N. Srivastava, Ann. of Math. 182 (2015) 307–325.
  • Example: for h = X² − 1, n = 2 and the points −2 < 0 < 2, the signs are +, −, +, so splits_of_alternating_sign shows that X² − 1 splits.
