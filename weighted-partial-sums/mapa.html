What the paper contributes

For a completely multiplicative ε : ℕ → {±1}, the weighted sum Sε(m) = Σn≤m n ε(n) can vanish only when m ≡ 0, 3 (mod 4).

  • Every admissible length is a zero (Theorem 1): for every such m ≥ 3 some ε has Sε(m) = 0 — a second moment, 104 integer certificates up to 108, and Sárközy’s progressions in subset sums beyond.
  • One fixed function rarely vanishes, but generically does (Theorem 5): the ε with infinitely many zeros form a dense Gδ of probability zero, since every finite prefix of signs extends to a zero (Proposition 4).
  • Quadratic characters are explicit points of that set: a digit recursion in base q (Theorem 6) propagates their zeros for q ≡ 1 (mod 4), for q ≡ 7 (mod 8) through the Euler factor at 2 of the weighted half sum (Theorem 7, classical in form), for class number one and along loops of the recursion (Corollary 8, Proposition 9); for odd characters of any order at (qj − 1)/2 exactly when χ(2) = 1 (Corollary 10).
  • The digits are Conrey’s null cells: they are the cells on which Conrey’s sine series vanishes identically; positivity of a related series would exclude them for q ≡ 3 (mod 8) (Proposition 11).
  • The origin: on the diagonal of the moment matrix of the ring of character values, the vanishing components are the odd characters trivial on ⟨2⟩, and the rank defect is d/t for every odd conductor (Theorem 14, known in substance: Jager–Lenstra, Girstmair).
  • Added after publication: for q = 179 and 227, χq,1 has no zero at all (N1), by a finite certificate checked in Lean.

🧭 Fields: where the paper lives and what it connects

Tools taken from other branches

  • Additive combinatorics → arithmetic progressions in subset sums (Sárközy, with Lev's constants in the form of Bringmann–Wellnitz); complete sequences and pairs — Theorem 1, Lemma 3
  • Probability → a second-moment bound; the anticoncentration theorem of Sárközy and Szemerédi; Borel–Cantelli — Lemma 2, Theorem 5
  • Topology (Baire category) → dense G_δ sets in the product space of sign patterns — Theorem 5
  • Algebraic number theory → generalized Bernoulli numbers B_{1,χ}, the class number formula of Jacobi and Dirichlet, Euler factors — Theorem 7, Corollary 8, Corollary 15
  • Representation theory of finite abelian groups → the idempotents e_χ of ℂ[G]: the rank of a cyclic module is the number of non-vanishing Fourier components — Lemma 12, Theorem 14
  • Formal verification → Lean 4 + Mathlib: the digit recursion and its corollaries, the weighted half sum, the subset-sum lemmas, the parity of the rows, and the finite certificate of N1 — Theorem 6, Corollary 8, Theorem 7, Lemma 3, Proposition 16, Theorem N1 (after publication)

What the results serve

  • Probabilistic number theory ← random multiplicative functions: with the weight n the sum jumps over 0, against the unweighted case (Aymone) — Theorem 1, Theorem 5
  • Analytic number theory (character sums) ← Conrey's sine series of the Legendre symbol, from his approach to the Riemann hypothesis: its null cells are the digits of the recursion — Corollary 8, Proposition 11
  • Partial sums of Legendre symbols ← the digit recursion of Borwein–Choi–Coons and Klurman–Mangerel–Pohoata–Teräväinen, with the weight added — Theorem 6, Corollary 8
  • Rings of character values (the companion paper) ← the components and the rank of the moment matrix on its diagonal, and which rows off it can be occupied — Lemma 12, Theorem 14, Corollary 17
