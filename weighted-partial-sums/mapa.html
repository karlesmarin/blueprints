What the paper contributes

For a completely multiplicative ε with values ±1, the weighted sum Sε(m) = Σn≤m n ε(n) can vanish only when m ≡ 0 or 3 (mod 4).

  • Every such length is a zero of some ε, depending on m (Theorem A), even with the signs at finitely many primes prescribed in advance, once m is large (Proposition 3.1).
  • Zeros of one fixed function are rare in measure and generic in category: the ε with infinitely many zeros form a dense Gδ of probability zero (Theorem B). For the unweighted sums the picture is the reverse: they vanish infinitely often almost surely, by a theorem of Aymone.
  • Quadratic characters give explicit zeros. For primes q ≡ 7 (mod 8), Sχ((qj−1)/2) = 0 for every j, and Conrey's sine series of the Legendre symbol vanishes identically on an interval (Corollary C). The case j = 1 is classical (Lebesgue; Aebi–Cairns); the iteration in j and the null interval are the corollary's content. They come from a digit recursion in base q (Theorem 4.1) and the weighted half sum (Theorem 4.2, due to Kanemitsu–Kuzumaki and Kučera).
  • Added after submission: for q = 179 and q = 227 the function χq,1 has no zero at all (Theorem N1), by a finite certificate checked in Lean.

Main tools: a second-moment bound and Sárközy's progressions in subset sums of primes (Theorem A); the anticoncentration theorem of Sárközy and Szemerédi (Theorem B).

Main results:

  • Theorem A: every admissible length is a zero of some ε
  • Theorem B: infinitely many zeros: dense G_δ, but measure zero
  • Corollary C: zeros of χ at (q^j−1)/2; Conrey's series vanishes on an interval
  • Theorem N1 (new): no zeros at all for q = 179 and q = 227

🧭 Fields: where the paper lives and what it connects

Tools taken from other branches

  • Additive combinatorics → arithmetic progressions in subset sums (Sárközy, with Lev's constants in the form of Bringmann–Wellnitz); complete sequences — Theorem A, Lemma 2.2
  • Probability → a second-moment bound; the anticoncentration theorem of Sárközy and Szemerédi — Lemma 2.1, Theorem B
  • Topology (Baire category) → dense Gδ sets in the product space of sign patterns — Theorem B
  • Algebraic number theory → the class number formula, through the weighted half sum (Kanemitsu–Kuzumaki, Kučera) — Theorem 4.2, Corollary C
  • Formal verification → Lean 4 + Mathlib: the digit recursion, its propagation step, complete sequences, and the finite certificate of Theorem N1 — Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.3, first statement, Theorem N1 (new)

What the results serve

  • Probabilistic number theory ← random multiplicative functions: with the weight n the picture of the unweighted sums (Aymone) is reversed — Theorem A, Theorem B
  • Analytic number theory (character sums) ← Conrey's sine series of the Legendre symbol, from his approach to the Riemann hypothesis: it vanishes on an interval for q ≡ 7 (mod 8); for q ≡ 3 (mod 8) the null cells are excluded under a positivity hypothesis — Corollary C, Proposition 4.6
  • Partial sums of Legendre symbols ← the digit recursion of Borwein–Choi–Coons and of Klurman et al., with the weight added — Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.3
