What the paper contributes

A column μ of the modular S-matrix of SU(n)k, normalised by its first entry, generates a ring Aμ of character values. Its field was known (Gannon–Schopieray); the paper determines the ring when the height p = n + k is prime.

  • The decomposition (Lemma 1, Proposition 2): Aμ = ℤ[ζm] ⊗ CT, a maximal cyclotomic factor fixed by the charge under the centre, and an order CT that depends only on the reduced spectrum T ⊂ 𝔽p; the index is a power of p.
  • The exact semigroup (Theorem 3, Corollary 5): locally at p, CT is a monomial ring whose valuations form a numerical semigroup ST, written by Gauss sums and Stickelberger's theorem; the index is pφ(m)δ, δ the number of gaps.
  • Read in 𝔽p (Theorem 4): when p > (n−1)(n−2), and for every p when n ≤ 12, ST is generated by e and the j with Mhj(T) ≠ 0.
  • One semigroup, every property (Corollaries 6–8, Propositions 9–10): Gorenstein ⇔ ST symmetric (Kunz), exponent p when the conductor is small, the vacuum is maximal, level–rank keeps ST, the conformal weight sees M2.
  • Counts and extremes (Propositions 11–12, §6): a closed count of the maximal columns, the largest defect (n−1)(n−2)/2 attained exactly by the trinomials Xn + aX + b, and the first non-Gorenstein column at prime height, SU(5)12.

Main results:

  • Proposition 2: the ring of a column is Z[ζ_m] ⊗ C_T, and its index is a power of p
  • Theorem 3: locally at p, C_T is the monomial ring of the numerical semigroup S_T, and δ(T) is its number of gaps
  • Theorem 4: when p > (n−1)(n−2), or n ≤ 12, the semigroup is read in F_p from the nonzero power sums M_{hj}(T)
  • Corollary 6: a column ring is Gorenstein if and only if its semigroup is symmetric

🧭 Fields: where the paper lives and what it connects

Tools taken from other branches

  • Gauss sums → Stickelberger's valuations of Gauss sums and Galois eigenprojectors in a tamely ramified extension — Theorem 3
  • Numerical semigroups → Selmer's formula for the genus, Schur's bound on the conductor (via Brauer), Kunz's Gorenstein criterion, Apéry sets — Theorem 3, Theorem 4, Corollary 6, Proposition 12
  • Symmetric functions → Newton's identities, complete symmetric functions, complementary partitions in a box (Macdonald) — Lemma 1, Theorem 4, Proposition 9
  • Polynomials over finite fields → Bank, Bary-Soroker and Rosenzweig: factorisation types in short intervals, for the existence of split trinomials — Proposition 12

What the results serve

  • Modular data and fusion rings ← the arithmetic of one column of the SU(n)_k S-matrix: index, Gorenstein property, level-rank duality, conformal weight — Corollary 5, Corollary 6, Proposition 9, Proposition 10
  • Orders generated by character values ← the order (not only the field) at an element of finite order of SU(n), in the line of Bächle and Sambale — Proposition 2, Corollary 5
  • The gluing of the columns (Part II) ← the defect δ(T) = number of gaps of S_T is the per-column term of the discriminant of the fusion ring — Theorem 3, Proposition 12
