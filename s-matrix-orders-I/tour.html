2 · In plain words

\paragraph{In plain words (commentary, not part of the paper).} 1. The ring $A_\mu$ of one column splits into two independent factors. The centre of $§U(n)$ contributes the integers $\ZZ[\zeta_m]$ of a cyclotomic field, fixed by the charge of $\mu$; that factor is already maximal and only multiplies the index. Everything else lives in the order $C_T$ generated by the elementary symmetric functions of $\zeta_p^t$, $t\in T$, which depends only on the reduced spectrum $T\subset\FF_p$. 2. Why: the fundamental characters at $g_\mu$ are $\eta_\mu^je_j(y_T)$, and Lemma 1 puts $\eta_\mu$ in the ring, so $A_\mu=\ZZ[\eta_\mu]\cdot C_T$; the two fields are linearly disjoint with coprime discriminants, which turns the product into a tensor product and the index into $[\cO_{E_T}:C_T]^{\varphi(m)}$. 3. The index is a power of $p$: at a prime $r\ne p$ the image of $C_T$ separates the geometric points of the étale algebra $\cO_{E_T}/r$, so it is all of it, and Nakayama gives maximality at $r$. From here on the paper studies only $\delta(T)=v_p[\cO_{E_T}:C_T]$.

3 · How it is proved

What the paper contributes

A column μ of the modular S-matrix of SU(n)k, normalised by its first entry, generates a ring Aμ of character values. Its field was known (Gannon–Schopieray); the paper determines the ring when the height p = n + k is prime.

  • The decomposition (Lemma 1, Proposition 2): Aμ = ℤ[ζm] ⊗ CT, a maximal cyclotomic factor fixed by the charge under the centre, and an order CT that depends only on the reduced spectrum T ⊂ 𝔽p; the index is a power of p.
  • The exact semigroup (Theorem 3, Corollary 5): locally at p, CT is a monomial ring whose valuations form a numerical semigroup ST, written by Gauss sums and Stickelberger's theorem; the index is pφ(m)δ, δ the number of gaps.
  • Read in 𝔽p (Theorem 4): when p > (n−1)(n−2), and for every p when n ≤ 12, ST is generated by e and the j with Mhj(T) ≠ 0.
  • One semigroup, every property (Corollaries 6–8, Propositions 9–10): Gorenstein ⇔ ST symmetric (Kunz), exponent p when the conductor is small, the vacuum is maximal, level–rank keeps ST, the conformal weight sees M2.
  • Counts and extremes (Propositions 11–12, §6): a closed count of the maximal columns, the largest defect (n−1)(n−2)/2 attained exactly by the trinomials Xn + aX + b, and the first non-Gorenstein column at prime height, SU(5)12.

It rests on:

  • Gauss sums — Stickelberger's valuations of Gauss sums and Galois eigenprojectors in a tamely ramified extension
  • Numerical semigroups — Selmer's formula for the genus, Schur's bound on the conductor (via Brauer), Kunz's Gorenstein criterion, Apéry sets
  • Symmetric functions — Newton's identities, complete symmetric functions, complementary partitions in a box (Macdonald)
  • Polynomials over finite fields — Bank, Bary-Soroker and Rosenzweig: factorisation types in short intervals, for the existence of split trinomials
