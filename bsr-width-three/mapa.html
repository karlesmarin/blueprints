What the paper contributes

The exact sum-of-squares rank of biquadratic forms of width three: every $m\times3$ biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of $2m$ squares of bilinear forms, and some need $2m$. This answers the question of Qi, Löfberg and Chen on the exact values of $\mathrm{BSR}(m,3)$; the cases $m=2,3$ were known, $m\ge4$ is new.

  • $\mathrm{BSR}(m,3)=2m$ for every $m\ge2$ (Theorem 1); in particular $\mathrm{BSR}(4,3)=8$.
  • An integer point where the map of $2m$ squares is a submersion, in every size (Lemma 2).
  • A rank inequality for Hankel multipliers without simple tensors: rank at least the support plus one (Lemma 5), replacing the Gorenstein classification of the boundary strategy of Blekherman, Dunbar and Sinn.
  • An explicit form needing $2m$ squares (Lemma 6).
